# CkProceduralAnimation

Procedural N-leg locomotion with independent surface motion and rigid N-joint leg presentation. The public ECS entry points are `UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE`, `UCk_Utils_ProceduralGait_UE`, `UCk_Utils_ProceduralRig_UE`, `UCk_Utils_ProceduralBodyPose_UE`, `UCk_Utils_SurfaceMotion_UE` and the one-call accelerant `UCk_Utils_ProceduralAnimation_UE::Add_Walker`; their generated AngelScript namespaces are `utils_procedural_leg`, `utils_procedural_gait`, `utils_procedural_rig`, `utils_procedural_body_pose`, `utils_surface_motion` and `utils_procedural_animation`.

## Composition

The body is a lifetime-owned entity with a transform. Legs are child entities of the body (`FCk_Handle_ProceduralLeg`), connected through `FFragment_RecordOfProceduralLegs`. The order is fixed: legs, then gait, then rigs and the optional body pose.

1. **Legs.** `UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::Create(Body, FCk_ProceduralLeg_Spec)` once per leg. A leg carries a unique `FName` Id, a body-local placement (hip, rest foot, phase offset, step-threshold scale) and its chain geometry (1..8 hip-first segment lengths in centimetres plus a body-local pole). The rig poses the chain; the gait reads the sum of its segment lengths as the leg's reach. Creating a leg on a body that already has a gait is rejected; the topology is fixed for the gait's lifetime.
2. **Surface motion (optional).** `UCk_Utils_SurfaceMotion_UE::Add(Body, FCk_SurfaceMotion_Spec)` when this module owns locomotion. Like leg `Create`, gait `Add` and `Add_Walker`, it rejects a body pending destruction. The Spec splits into `_Contact` (clearance, probe reach, contact grace, confirm angle 30 degrees, confirm time 0.075 s, query filter) and `_Movement` (max speed, surface turn rate, clearance speed, gravity, steer floor 0.4); `Add` also rejects a confirm angle outside 0..180 degrees, a negative or non-finite confirm time and a steer floor outside 0..1. The accepted support frame is independent of the body's interpolated orientation. `Request_Steering` takes a world direction and speed; the direction is projected onto the accepted surface tangent, unless that projection is shorter than the steer floor's share of the direction: on a face nearly perpendicular to the steering (a log's lower facets, a wall under a horizontal route) the projection is a short vector whose direction flips from facet to facet, so the body keeps its travel tangent laid onto the new plane instead. Each update runs 16 ms substeps of the pure `ck::StepProceduralSurfaceMotion` (`Core/CkProceduralSurfaceMotion.h`), which casts its rays through a Jolt adapter:
   - **Rays.** Forward from the body within the clearance; down from one clearance above the body for the probe reach; a look-ahead down ray from 1.5 clearances ahead, which counts only on a surface within 15 degrees of the support normal; and, around a convex edge, a fan back and down from one clearance ahead. The forward hit wins, then the down hit. The look-ahead replaces a down hit that lies below the clearance when its own hit lies more than a quarter clearance higher: after the fan turns the body onto a ledge or beam top one clearance behind the edge, the down ray sees the lower floor, and following it would sink the body back below the edge. After a down miss the look-ahead comes before the fan. The forward ray and the fan are cast only while the body moves; the down ray and the look-ahead run at any speed, so a body that stops within a clearance past a crest keeps the top it stands at instead of sinking to the floor below or falling. `Get_ContactSource` reports which ray the body's last accepted contact came from (`ECk_SurfaceMotion_ContactSource`: `None` while no ray hits, `Forward`, `Down`, `LookAhead`, `Fan`, `Fall`).
   - **Confirmation.** A contact whose normal turns more than the confirm angle from the support while the down ray still hits is adopted only once it has been seen for the confirm time, each sighting within 15 degrees of the last. Until then the body keeps the support under it, or coasts along its plane when the down ray itself proposed the turn, and `Get_ContactQuery` stays `Trusted`; a coasting substep adopts nothing, so `Get_ContactSource` keeps the source it last accepted. A face the rays graze for a few substeps (a rock side under a tilted down ray, a riser top edge at clearance height) is never adopted. The body is not stopped while it waits, so a body longer than its clearance can overrun a head-on wall by up to its speed times the confirm time (13.5 cm at 180 cm/s) before it turns. A down ray that hits nothing adopts any contact at once, so convex edges turn as soon as the fan finds the next face; a face the down ray meets along its length but does not trust (grazed along the seam two solids share, or facing away) still counts as support for the confirmation, so a seam cannot fast-track a large turn, while a down ray that starts on or inside a solid tells nothing about the ground below and counts as a miss.
   - **Contact loss.** Without a trusted hit the body coasts for the contact grace, then falls under gravity along a swept ray. The landing keeps the heading the body travelled with: its travel tangent laid onto the landing plane, then the steering, then its own forward.
   - `Get_Velocity` is the last substep's velocity.
3. **Gait.** `UCk_Utils_ProceduralGait_UE::Add(Body, GaitData)` requires 2..64 legs already connected. It captures the legs in record order; that order is the solver's leg index and never changes. `Get_Legs(Body)` returns the record's current order, which changes after a detach; address legs by `Get_Id` or keep the handles `Create` returned. Tuning comes from a `UCk_ProceduralGait_Data` preset (timing, step, probe, foothold), copied at `Add`; `Add`, `Request_ApplyPreset` and `Add_Walker` reject a preset any of whose four parts is malformed. Every captured leg's rest foot must lie within the step's `_TargetReachFraction` of its reach from its hip; `Add`, `Request_ApplyPreset` and `Add_Walker` reject a layout or preset that breaks this atomically (see **Reach and cadence**). The gait consumes body motion and performs passive Jolt queries. It does not translate the body.
4. **Rigs.** `UCk_Utils_ProceduralRig_UE::Add(Leg, FCk_ProceduralRig_Spec)` binds caller-owned transform entities to one leg, hip-first, plus an optional foot. The segment count must equal the leg's segment-length count. Every part must carry a transform, be a unique direct lifetime child of the leg's body, and not be bound by another leg's rig. Segment geometry must be centred on its transform origin with length along local +X. Omit the foot with an empty handle; a stale supplied handle is an error. `_Solver` (`ECk_ProceduralRig_ChainSolver`) picks how the chain is posed: `Auto` aims a single segment from the hip toward the foot target at the segment's own length, uses two-bone IK for two segments and the curve for three or more; an explicit `Fabrik` or `Curve` applies to any chain of two or more, and `Add` rejects either on a single segment. The curve (`ck::SolveProceduralLegCurve`) lays the links hip-first along a planar cubic Bezier from hip to foot, bent toward the pole, and picks the arch height that ends the chain on the foot; FABRIK is seeded half along the pole, then straight at the target. The curve core rejects a bend along the hip-foot line instead of picking a side, so the rig then bends toward the body's up, then its right. A folded chain, whose foot comes within about 40 % of its length of the hip, can have no arch that closes on the foot (chains of few, uneven links open that gap earliest); the chain then ends off the foot. The reach clamp keeps gait targets short of full extension, where every solver straightens abruptly. `_Clearance` (`ECk_ProceduralRig_Clearance`, default `None`) opts the rig into keeping its posed chain out of solids by swivelling the knee (see **Chain clearance**).
5. **Body pose (optional).** `UCk_Utils_ProceduralBodyPose_UE::Add(Gait, FCk_ProceduralBodyPose_Spec)` makes the drawn body sag and tilt toward the legs that no longer hold it up. The Spec names a caller-owned presentation entity (`_Presentation`: a live transform entity that is a direct lifetime child of the body, not the body itself and not a rig part) and receives `Offset * Body` every frame, where `Offset` is body-local; attach the body's visual there, not to the body. The target offset comes from the pure `ck::ComputeProceduralBodySupportPose` over the gait's captured legs: a leg supports unless it is disabled, detached or destroyed, the body drops `_Support._CollapseDrop` centimetres times the unsupported fraction of captured legs, and it tilts up to `_Support._MaxTilt` degrees about the horizontal axis perpendicular to the gap between the centroid of all captured hips and the centroid of the supporting hips, dipping the unsupported side. `Add` captures the hip of every captured leg into `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_SupportLayout`, so it requires every leg the gait captured to still be live and rejects a gait that has already lost one; a leg detached or destroyed later keeps its captured hip, so the body tilts toward it exactly as toward the same leg disabled. `Offset` follows the target through the engine's `UKismetMathLibrary::VectorSpringInterp` and `QuaternionSpringInterp` (`_Spring`: stiffness, critical damping factor, mass and `_MaxAttitudeLag`). `_Conform._Mode = PlantedFeet` also tilts and lifts the target toward the planted feet (see **Body conform**); the target the springs follow is the conform rotation times the support-loss rotation, at the support-loss drop plus the conform height. Every frame, before springing, the body pose splits the simulation body's rotation since the last frame into a swing (tilt) and a twist about the body's up, and carries the inverse swing into the offset, so the drawn body keeps its world tilt when the body's attitude steps (SurfaceMotion's rate-capped support rotation steps at every facet it crosses) and the springs then ease it to the new target; the yaw passes straight through. This applies with or without conform. `_Spring._MaxAttitudeLag` (default 22 degrees, `Add` rejects a negative value) limits only how much of each frame's swing is carried: all of it while the offset, without this frame's carry, trails its target by less than the max lag minus an 8-degree knee (the knee shrinks to the max lag when that is smaller), none of it at the max lag, a linear share between. A facet step is smoothed in full; on a sustained rotation such as a ramp-to-wall corner, which the critically damped spring alone would trail by twice the rate over its natural frequency (over 60 degrees through a 90-degree corner turned at SurfaceMotion's 240 degrees per second), the lag settles below the max lag and the drawn body's rate ramps up to the body's across the knee without a step. Nothing but the springs moves the offset toward its target, so a target that jumps (the support pose on losing legs) is eased like any other; the conform part of the target is rate-limited before the springs see it (see **Body conform**). 0 carries no swing: the drawn body turns with the body and the springs ease it. `Get_TargetOffset` returns the body-local target the springs followed on the last update, with the conform part slewed (identity unless `Ready`). The simulation body is untouched: probes, the gait and SurfaceMotion never see the sag, so released debris cannot become ground. The rig composes hips and pole from `UCk_Utils_ProceduralBodyPose_UE::Get_Offset(Body) * Body`, so the legs stay attached to the drawn body while the feet stay where the gait put them; `Get_Offset` is identity unless the body pose is `Ready`, so a body without a pose, or with a failed one, keeps its hips on the body. Losing the presentation entity latches `ECk_ProceduralBodyPose_Failure::MissingPresentation`. The body pose keeps following the body after the gait latches a failure: its update excludes the gait's setup tag but not its failure fragment, because the presentation carries the body's visual. Lean from acceleration and impulses for hit reactions are not implemented.
6. **Readiness.** Gait, rig and surface motion each report `Get_Status() -> ECk_ProceduralAnimation_Status` (`PendingSetup`, `Ready`, `Failed`); wait for `Ready` on gait and rig. Body pose has no setup and reads `Ready` or `Failed`; `ProceduralBodyPose.Get_Offset` and `Get_TargetOffset` are identity unless it is `Ready`. A rig reads `PendingSetup` until its body's gait is `Ready`. Observe `Failed` rather than treating permanent failure as delayed readiness; `Get_Failure()` names the reason (`ECk_ProceduralGait_Failure`, `ECk_ProceduralRig_Failure`, `ECk_ProceduralBodyPose_Failure`, `ECk_SurfaceMotion_Failure`; `None` while not failed). An invalid handle or a handle without the feature reads `Failed`. Ready means set up, not grounded. `ProceduralGait.Get_PlantedCount` counts disabled legs as planted, because their frozen foot is planted. `SurfaceMotion.Get_Support` returns `Grounded` through the contact-grace interval; `Get_ContactQuery` reports whether the current query was `Trusted` or `Missed`, and `Get_ContactSource` which ray it came from. The reflected foot reports `ECk_ProceduralLeg_FootPhase` (`Planted`/`Swinging`), `ECk_ProceduralLeg_FootContact` (`Trusted`/`Guessed`) and `ECk_ProceduralLeg_Foothold`, where this solve's target came from (`Ideal`, `Held`, or a search source `Front`, `Inward`, `Outward`, `Ring`; `None` when nothing was usable, and on a disabled leg; see **Foothold search**); `ProceduralLeg.Get_IdealVerdict` reports why that solve's ideal target was or was not usable (`ECk_ProceduralLeg_FootholdVerdict`, the reflected mirror of the gait's verdict; a disabled leg keeps its last one); `ProceduralLeg.Get_EnableDisable` reports the leg's `ECk_EnableDisable`. Gait, rig, body-pose and surface-motion failures latch: recovery requires replacing the composition. The rigid rig requires a unit-scale body; author segment lengths and part geometry at the intended size.

**Data assets.** `UCk_ProceduralGait_Data` is a tuning preset with no legs, reusable across creatures. `UCk_ProceduralRig_Data` is the leg layout for one creature: per-leg placement and chain geometry, no runtime handles. Both validate in the editor through `IsDataValid`. Values are copied into the runtime fragments at `Add`; editing the asset during play does not retune a live gait.

**Fragments.** Each feature's primary state fragment is its membership anchor: `Has` and `Cast` key on `ck::FFragment_ProceduralLeg`, `ck::FFragment_ProceduralGait`, `ck::FFragment_ProceduralRig` and `ck::FFragment_SurfaceMotion` alone. `Add` places `ck::FTag_ProceduralGait_NeedsSetup`, `ck::FTag_ProceduralRig_NeedsSetup` or `ck::FTag_SurfaceMotion_NeedsSetup`; the feature's `Setup` processor consumes it and every later processor excludes it. A latched failure is a data fragment (`ck::FFragment_ProceduralGait_Failure`, `ck::FFragment_ProceduralRig_Failure`, `ck::FFragment_SurfaceMotion_Failure`) whose presence excludes the entity from `Update` and whose field is the reason; readiness is derived from those two presences, never stored. SurfaceMotion splits steering intent (`ck::FFragment_SurfaceMotion`: direction and speed, the only data `Request_Steering` writes) from the accepted support frame (`ck::FFragment_SurfaceMotion_Support`, which holds the core's `ck::FProceduralSurfaceMotionState`: support normal, travel tangent, velocity, missing-contact time, grounded and trusted-contact flags, contact source and the contact pending confirmation, rewritten every substep). `ck::FFragment_ProceduralLeg` holds the published foot and the ideal target's verdict, both written by the gait. A disabled leg carries `ck::FFragment_ProceduralLeg_FrozenPose`. Body pose keeps the presentation, spring and support of its Spec in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_Params`, its body-local offset, the body-local target offset the springs last followed, the two engine spring states and the body rotation the offset was last carried to in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose` (the membership anchor), the captured legs' hips in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_SupportLayout`, a latched failure in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_Failure`, and, only when it conforms, the conform tunables in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_Conform` and, in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_ConformState`, the last fitted target and the applied target the springs chase; it places no setup tag because the engine springs initialise themselves on their first step. `_EvaluatedFrame` (SurfaceMotion) and `_PosedSolveSequence` (rig) record the frame and solve each feature last produced output for; the debugger reads them to judge freshness. `ck::FFragment_ProceduralRig` also keeps the posed joints and, for a rig with a clearance policy, the stored swivel angle, the chain state and the crossing-link count of its last pose. Leg, rig and surface motion read every Spec field at steady state and never mutate it, so their config fragments alias the Spec (`FFragment_ProceduralLeg_Params = FCk_ProceduralLeg_Spec`, likewise for rig and surface motion). The gait's timing, step, probe and foothold live in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralGait_Tunables`, which `Request_ApplyPreset` replaces. `ck::FFragment_ProceduralGait::_Footholds` holds each leg's held foothold (`ck::FProceduralFootholdState`: world position, normal, source, whether one is held, how it was found and the solve its last fruitless search ran on), sized with `_Probes` at `Add`; `_NextSearchLeg` is the leg the next update's search budget starts from. `ck::FFragment_ProceduralGait_Debug` holds the last accepted snapshot, its per-leg scratch, the reach cadence floor and skipped-leg count of the last solver-settings rebuild, and `_RaysLastSolve`, the rays the last setup or update cast, to which the rigs with a clearance policy add their link rays after the update; the gait's `Add`, its setup, its request drain and its update write it, the rig update adds to its ray count, and only `UCk_Utils_ProceduralAnimation_Debug_UE` reads it. The gait's `_SolveSequence` counts accepted solves: the rig stamps it and the snapshot copies it, so no production processor reads debug data. Solver inputs and outputs are per-tick locals of the gait update. Plain `ck::` types that are neither fragments nor tags, such as `FProceduralGaitBuiltSettings`, the solver-settings build result, live in the feature's `_Fragment_Data.h` with the reflected types.

**Accelerant.** `UCk_Utils_ProceduralAnimation_UE::Add_Walker(Body, RigData, GaitData, Chains)` creates one leg per layout entry (asset order becomes record order), adds the gait, then binds each supplied `FCk_ProceduralWalker_LegChain` (leg Id + rig chain). It validates everything before the first mutation and returns an empty `FCk_ProceduralWalker` on rejection. An empty chain list yields a gait-only walker.

The module creates no render components or assets. Callers can use CkPmg or CkIsmRenderer for rigid parts; a future skeletal adapter can consume the same world foot outputs (`UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::Get_Foot`: position, normal, rotation, swing alpha, phase, contact, foothold source).

## Chain clearance

`FCk_ProceduralRig_Spec::_Clearance` decides whether the rig keeps its posed chain out of solids and out of its drawn body. `None`, the default, poses the chain toward the leg's authored pole and casts no ray. `Swivel`:

- **Link test.** After the chain is posed, by any solver, each link (joint i to joint i + 1, from the hip to the foot) is tested with one ray using the body gait's `_Probe._QueryFilter`; the rig reads the gait's tunables on its body, which always has a gait. A hit with fraction in (0.02, 0.98) crosses a solid; a fraction-0 hit crosses too, because the link starts inside one; a hit within the last 2 % is the surface the next joint stands on and does not.
- **Body slab.** The link rays see only solids, and a drawn body may have no collider, so the rig also keeps the chain's interior joints (every joint but the hip and the foot) out of a slab standing in for the body: the box of the hips of every leg the gait captured, in the drawn body's frame (the body pose's offset times the body), 5 cm wider in X and Y and a quarter of the posed leg's rest drop above and below the hips. A link with an interior joint inside the slab counts as crossing. The slab casts no ray.
- **Swivel fan.** When a link crosses, the chain is posed again with the pole rotated about the hip-to-foot line by each angle of `ck::ProceduralPoleSwivelFanDegrees` (0, 30, -30, 60, -60, 90, -90, 120, -120, 150, -150; the wide tiers last, so they are tried only when every narrower angle crosses), in the order `ck::Get_ProceduralPoleSwivelOrder` gives: the leg's last clear angle first, then 0, then the fan's other angles in fan order. Each candidate is tested the same way. The first pose clear of solids and of the slab is kept and its angle stored; when none is, the pose with the fewest crossing links is kept (ties to the smaller absolute angle) and the stored angle becomes 0. There the authored pole (angle 0) counts only the links its rays find crossing, so a rig with the policy never poses worse than one without. The pure `ck::ComputeProceduralPoleSwivel` (`Core/CkProceduralChainClearance.h`, no world) rotates the pole right-handed about hip->foot. Every solver receives the swivelled pole exactly as it receives the authored one: the aim ignores it, two-bone IK takes it as its joint target, FABRIK seeds half the chain along it, the curve bends toward it; the segments' roll follows it too. The foot never moves: the swivel changes the knee, not the target.
- **Continuity.** The stored angle is tried first on the next solve, so a knee that went round a pillar stays there while the pillar is in the way and returns to 0 once its angle stops being clear and 0 is (0 is always tried second). The angle is not blended: the knee jumps between fan angles, and a 30-degree jump is visible at 60 Hz. Blending the angle is the recorded follow-up.
- **Limits.** A link whose far end lies inside a solid, such as a swinging foot passing through a pillar, crosses at every angle; so does a chain whose only clear bends lie more than 150 degrees from the authored pole. Both keep the pose with the fewest crossing links and read `Crossing`. The link test sees only what the probe's filter sees in the Jolt world, and the slab is only a box through the hips: a drawn body that reaches beyond its hips is covered only as far as the margins, and the authored pole's pose is not slab-tested once nothing clears.
- **Cost.** Per rigged leg per frame: one ray per link while the stored angle stays clear, up to eleven per link while the chain crosses. The rays count toward the gait's `Get_RaysLastSolve`, which the rig adds to after the gait update in the same frame.
- **Failure and readouts.** A chain no angle clears is a diagnostic, not a failure: no failure reason exists for it. `UCk_Utils_ProceduralRig_UE::Get_Clearance` returns the policy, `Get_ChainState` (`ECk_ProceduralRig_ChainState`: `Clear`, `Crossing`; always `Clear` without a policy, which tests nothing) whether the last pose kept a link through a solid or into the slab, and `Get_SwivelDegrees` the stored angle.

The gait's straight hip-to-foot occlusion line (see **Foothold search**) stays a veto on its targets: the rig clears the chain to a target the gait chose, it does not widen the targets the gait may choose.

## Disabling a leg

`UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::Request_EnableDisable` is reversible. A disabled leg leaves the step schedule and its foot freezes body-relative: the gait captures the foot pose in body space on the transition into `ck::FFragment_ProceduralLeg_FrozenPose`, present only while the leg is disabled, and re-expresses it every frame, so the rig keeps posing the leg and it rides rigidly with the body. Re-enabling removes the fragment. Disabled legs cost no Jolt queries. Survivors keep their indices; nothing is packed or re-indexed.

## Losing a leg

`UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::Request_Detach` is irreversible. In the leg's request drain it collects the parts the rig was posing, optionally transfers their lifetime ownership to the world (`ECk_ProceduralLeg_ReleasedPartsOwnership::TransferToWorld`), fires `OnProceduralLeg_Detached(Leg, ReleasedParts)` while the leg handle is still readable, then destroys the leg entity. The drain runs before the gait and rig updates, so released parts never receive another pose request. The gait masks the lost index and keeps running on the survivors.

Ownership stays with the body unless `TransferToWorld` is requested: destroying the body then destroys body-owned debris.

Ragdolling the released parts is the game's job. The recipe:

1. Bind `BindTo_OnDetached` on the leg, or read `UCk_Utils_ProceduralRig_UE::Get_Chain` first.
2. Call `Request_Detach`.
3. In the callback, for each released part: add a dynamic Jolt body with an explicit box shape sized like the part's visual (`FCk_JoltBody_Spec{ECk_JoltBody_ShapeSource::ExplicitShape}` with `FCk_Jolt_ShapeDimensions{ECk_Jolt_ShapeType::Box}` half-extents, `ECk_MotionType::Dynamic`, an explicit mass, then `UCk_Utils_JoltBody_UE::Add`). CkJolt does not read CkShapes. Give the body a collision profile the gait and surface-motion probes do not trace: the gym uses `Ragdoll` (PhysicsBody objects that ignore the Visibility channel the probes use). Released debris must never read as ground for the survivors.
4. Optionally push the parts with `UCk_Utils_JoltBody_UE::Request_AddImpulse` — only once `UCk_Utils_JoltBody_UE::Get_IsBodyAdded` is true, because an impulse before the body exists is dropped. The gym fixture (`CkProceduralAnimationGym_Fixture.as`) is the reference implementation of the Jolt body and the impulse. A CkTimer that destroys the debris later is an optional addition; the fixture does not add one.
5. Bind `UCk_Utils_ProceduralGait_UE::BindTo_OnLegSetChanged` to react to the new leg count: switch locomotion mode, or `Request_ApplyPreset` with a request built from a slower preset.

**Leg-loss policy.** `FCk_ProceduralGait_Timing::_LegLossPolicy` decides how survivors adapt. `KeepAuthoredOffsets` (default) keeps every leg's authored phase. `RedistributeOffsets` re-spaces the enabled legs evenly by index and blends there through the solver's pattern-blend machinery. With `_MaxSimultaneousSwings = 0` the swing budget is `max(1, enabled legs / 2)`, recomputed whenever the enabled set changes.

**Signals and live retune.** `OnProceduralGait_LegSetChanged(Gait, EnabledCount, TotalCount)` tracks the enabled set: it fires on the frame a leg is disabled, enabled, or detached or destroyed while enabled. Detaching or destroying an already-disabled leg leaves the enabled set unchanged and does not fire it. `TotalCount` is the number of legs the gait captured at `Add`, not the number still attached. `UCk_Utils_ProceduralGait_UE::Request_ApplyPreset` takes an `FCk_Request_ProceduralGait_ApplyPreset` (timing, step, probe and foothold; build it from a `UCk_ProceduralGait_Data` preset's `Get_Timing`, `Get_Step`, `Get_Probe` and `Get_Foothold`), copies those into the live gait and re-applies solver settings; leg state, planted feet, held footholds and the gait clock are kept.

## Footfall events

A leg fires `OnProceduralLeg_Planted(Leg, Footfall)` when its published foot phase changes to `Planted` and `OnProceduralLeg_Lifted(Leg, Footfall)` when it changes to `Swinging`; bind with `UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::BindTo_OnPlanted` / `BindTo_OnLifted` (default policy `FireIfPayloadInFlightThisFrame`, `DoNothing`) and unbind with the `UnbindFrom_*` twins. Only the gait update fires them, right after it writes the leg's foot, so a plant and a lift alternate per leg and each change fires once. Gait setup seeds every foot as planted without firing. `FCk_ProceduralLeg_Footfall` carries the foot's world position and normal, its `ECk_ProceduralLeg_FootContact`, and `_LandingSpeed`: the foot's speed in cm/s over the frame it planted, 0 on a lift. There is no physical material: the Jolt hit carries none. Disabling a swinging leg freezes its foot as planted, and that transition fires a plant with a guessed contact; re-enabling fires nothing until the foot next lifts. A detached leg never fires: it is pending destruction from its request drain on, and the gait freezes its last pose without publishing a footfall.

## Body conform

`FCk_ProceduralBodyPose_Spec::_Conform` (`FCk_ProceduralBodyPose_Conform`: `_Mode`, `_MaxTilt` 20 degrees, `_HeightWeight` 0.5, `_MaxHeight` 10 cm, `_MaxTiltRate` 60 degrees per second, `_MaxHeightRate` 60 cm per second) is unpacked at `Add`: `PlantedFeet` adds the conform fragments, `None` adds neither, and `Add` rejects a max tilt outside 0..89 degrees, a height weight outside 0..1, a negative max height, or a max tilt rate or max height rate that is not finite and positive. Every frame the pure `ck::ComputeProceduralBodyConformPose` fits a weighted least-squares plane `h = a x + b y + c` through each captured foot's height above its rest height, at the foot's x and y in the simulation body's frame. A planted foot counts fully; a swinging foot fades out over the first third of its swing and back in over the last third, so the fit does not step when the planted set changes; a disabled, detached or destroyed leg counts zero, and so does every foot while the gait is airborne, because tucked feet stand on nothing (the fit is then underdetermined and the last one holds). The fit tilts the target's up onto the plane's normal, clamped to `_MaxTilt`, with no turn about the body's up, and moves it `_HeightWeight` times `c` along the body's up, clamped to `_MaxHeight`. The core returns `Fitted`, `Underdetermined` (fewer than three weighted feet, or weighted feet along one line: the update holds the last fitted target in the body's frame) or `Malformed` (a non-finite foot or a negative weight: the update ensures and latches `ECk_ProceduralBodyPose_Failure::MalformedConform`). A two-group walker with four legs holds during every swing and refits while all four stand. The target the springs chase is not the held fit itself but an applied target that the pure `ck::SlewProceduralBodyConformPose` moves toward it every frame, turning at most `_MaxTiltRate` and moving at most `_MaxHeightRate`: with feet on two faces of a sharp edge the plane swaps by up to 36 degrees in one frame, and the springs alone would still read that as a lurch. The critically damped rotation spring trails a target turning at a steady rate r by about 2 r / ω, with ω = sqrt(`_Spring._Stiffness` / `_Spring._Mass`), so keep 2 × `_MaxTiltRate` / ω within `_Spring._MaxAttitudeLag`: at the default stiffness 40 and mass 1, ω is 6.32 per second and a target slewing at the default 60 degrees per second is trailed by about 19 degrees, against the default 22. Both targets are body-local and live in the conform state fragment. The fit works in the body's frame, so it behaves the same on walls and ceilings, and the support-loss sag stays toward the surface the feet stand on; a game that wants gravity sag drives its own offset.

## Reach and cadence

A leg's reach is the sum of its segment lengths. `FCk_ProceduralGait_Step` carries three fractions of it, validated as `0 < _TargetReachFraction < _ForceStepReachFraction <= 1` and `_ForceStepReachFraction <= _HardOverstretchReachFraction`, which lies in `[1, 1.5]` (defaults 0.8, 0.92 and 1.0); the solver holds them in `ck::FProceduralGaitReachSettings`. A step whose force-step and hard-overstretch fractions are both 1 is valid: every reach Emergency then also steps beyond the schedule.

- **Target clamp.** Every swing target stays within `_TargetReachFraction` of the hip: the take-off target (a catch step keeps its requested target, clamped), the damped retarget, the push at the retarget freeze phase, the target after the stroke overshoot (every frame, against the current hip), the swing a re-enabled leg begins, and the landing after airborne. `ck::FProceduralGaitSolver::ClampToReach` shrinks the planar offset in the support frame and keeps the height; a height alone beyond the limit clamps in 3D. Before probing, the gait clamps its query point (`Neutral + Lead`, with the velocity lead projected onto the support plane) the same way, so the probe looks for ground under a reachable point.
- **Ground within reach.** A probe hit farther than `_TargetReachFraction` of the reach from the hip is clamped the same way and probed once more there, so the target the solver receives is real ground within reach. The solver's clamp keeps a target's support-frame height, which across a convex bend (a crest, a ridge, a stair edge) lies inside the ground; the second probe keeps that clamp to trimming the stroke overshoot and the freeze push.
- **Unreachable ground holds the plant.** A final hit still farther than `_ForceStepReachFraction` of the reach from the hip is Unreachable, like a miss: the foothold search runs (see **Foothold search**); when it finds nothing, the plant holds through the contact grace, then gathers toward its rest target. The debug probe shows the final attempt. A foot never plants on ground far below a reachable point, as at a ledge or a gap. A hit whose normal faces along the ray (a back face) is never trusted either.
- **Reach Emergency.** A planted foot farther than `_ForceStepReachFraction` of the reach from its hip is an Emergency, joining the error Emergency (`Error > Threshold × EmergencyFactor`) with its rules: it needs a valid target, ignores the swing window and the budget, and waits for inhibition (unless it is also hard-overstretched, below). A planted foot whose hip cannot see it (the leg input's `_PlantOccluded`) is an Emergency of ratio 1, so an over-reach or a large error still outranks it in the priority choice; with a valid target it steps beyond the schedule like a hard-overstretched foot (below).
- **Priority.** The planted Emergency leg whose phase group has no swing in flight, with the largest ratio `max(Error / (Threshold × EmergencyFactor), |Plant − Hip| / (ForceStepFraction × Reach))`, holds back every take-off in the other groups, their Emergency take-offs included, until it steps. Without it an Emergency leg starves: while a group swings, inhibition keeps it planted, and on the frame that group drains, leg index order hands the slot to another group. While the body turns in place every group is in Emergency at once, so exempting Emergency take-offs would starve one group for seconds. Catch steps of other groups are not held back and inhibition is unchanged, so the schedule's own take-offs never overlap two groups.
- **Hard overstretch.** A planted foot farther than `_HardOverstretchReachFraction` of the reach from its hip, with a valid target, steps beyond the phase schedule: it takes off although another group swings or holds priority. A body that climbs away from its planted feet (up a pillar, onto a beam end or a ledge) stretches every group's floor feet at once, and the schedule would step them one group after another; a 16-leg centipede's last group waited a whole cycle, 0.3 to 0.4 s, up to 1.6 chain lengths out. The swings it starts form a second lane beside the schedule, marked `FProceduralGaitLegSwing::_BeyondSchedule`: they neither inhibit the schedule's take-offs nor wait for them, and only one phase group swings in the lane at a time, so at most two groups ever swing, one on the schedule and one beyond it. A take-off beyond the schedule needs the swing budget, unlike an Emergency on it; a lane swing still counts toward the budget and still keeps its group out of the priority choice. A pending catch step waits for the schedule as before. An occluded plant with a valid target takes off in the same lane under the same budget and one-group rule: every frame it waits, the body carries it deeper behind the solid, and at travel speed the other group's reach Emergencies would hold its ratio of 1 back for longer than a step. The rule cannot step a foot whose target the contact grace withholds: the floor below a raised hip usually lies beyond the force-step reach, so a floor foot holds through the grace first. At the default of 1 the rule never fires on a straight walk, where planted feet stay within 0.94 of their chains at up to 260 cm/s.
- **Turning.** The gait reads the body's turn rate from the twist, about the body's up, of its frame-to-frame rotation (`ck::FProceduralGaitYawRateTracker`), so a tilt step at a facet or a wall corner reads as no turn; the rate is signed and low-passed over 0.1 s. Its magnitude times the mean rest-foot radius adds to the cadence speed. Its sign drives the yaw term of Raibert placement: before the reach clamp, the query point `Neutral + Lead` is turned about the body location by the rate times `_StepDuration`, the same time the linear lead uses, so a turning body's feet land ahead of the turn; the whole lead, travel and turn together, then moves the query point at most `_MaxVelocityLead` from its neutral (`ck::FProceduralGaitSolver::ComputeLeadQuery`). The cap keeps a fast turner's lead in proportion (240 degrees per second times a 0.35 s step is 84 degrees) and bounds what a one-frame heading jump, such as SurfaceMotion's re-seat after a fall, can do to the targets while the low-passed rate carries it. The push at the retarget freeze still uses the planar velocity alone and ignores the turn.
- **Ground under the landing point.** On every swing frame the core exposes the point the swing will land on, after the stroke overshoot and the reach clamp (`FProceduralGaitLegSwing::_LandingPoint`): on the take-off frame, the swing target; before the retarget freeze, the point the freeze will produce from this frame's ideal target advanced by one frame of planar travel, because the damped target trails a moving ideal, the freeze snaps it forward (by about 13 cm at 180 cm/s) and the probe's report reaches the solver a frame later; from the freeze on, the actual landing point. The freeze and this prediction share one solver function. The gait probes under that point like the clearance probe, along the support down from `_Probe._Up` above, trusting no back face and no hit farther than `_ForceStepReachFraction` of the reach from the hip, and passes the hit's height as the leg input `_LandingGroundZ` on the next frame. A swing whose target normal lies more than 45 degrees from the support up (a foothold on a face) is not probed: a face has no tread to lift onto. When that ground lies above the landing target and the lifted point stays within `_TargetReachFraction` of the reach, the swing lifts onto it: the rest of the arc rises by the difference in step with the swing's own easing, from the alpha the lift began at, so the foot reaches the upper tread at touchdown without a jump. A lifted swing follows the latest report, down to no lift when a later report finds no ground above, so a landing point that moves back off the upper tread never plants hovering. A probe that finds nothing it trusts (a miss, or ground beyond the force-step reach, as over a gap) is no report: a lifted swing then keeps its last lift. A catch step never lifts. The overshoot and the freeze push can carry a target probed on a lower tread past the next riser, which is what this lifts out of the upper tread. A report that first arrives on the touchdown frame lifts the plant only when the rise is at most `FProceduralGaitReachSettings::_TouchdownLiftFraction` of the step height (0.08 by default, 2 cm at a 25 cm step; `ValidateSettings` keeps it in `[0, 1]`); a larger one is left below that ground and counted (`FProceduralGaitSolver::Get_MissedLandingLifts`, also in the debug snapshot). The plant keeps the target's ground normal, which is right on a tread and wrong on a slope. The debug snapshot records each leg's landing-ground probe and the point it was cast under.
- **Cadence floor.** For each enabled leg, with `L` its reach, `dz` the hip's height over the rest foot and `lateral` the rest foot's body-Y offset from the hip: `R_t = sqrt(max(0, (TargetFraction × L)² − dz²))` and `R_f` likewise with `ForceStepFraction`. The leg strides along body X over the chord `U = sqrt(R_t² − lateral²) + sqrt(R_f² − lateral²)`, and is skipped when `lateral >= R_t`. With `Ts = CycleDuration − StepDuration`, the solver runs with `min(authored CadenceSpeedRef, 0.85 × min U / Ts)`, which keeps stance travel per cycle within 85 % of the shortest chord. The floor is rebuilt at `Add`, on `Request_ApplyPreset` and whenever the enabled set changes. An authored reference of 0 (cadence scaling off) stays 0; `Ts <= 0` or no striding leg leaves the authored value. Admission keeps every rest foot inside the target radius, so a skipped leg can only sit exactly on it. The chord ignores the velocity lead: a swing lands at most `_MaxVelocityLead` ahead of the rest foot, so a lead shorter than the chord's forward part leaves a shorter usable stride and a floor more permissive than the chord suggests. For the gym's walkers the floor is above the authored reference, so it changes nothing there; it guards layouts whose legs cannot cover the stance travel.

## Foothold search

Every enabled leg chooses its foot target this way on every update, and once at setup so a search pick is held from the first update on. Down rays are the ideal probe's ray under a point (`_Probe._Up` above to `_Probe._Down` below along the trace axis, leaned outward on the first attempt); every hit is validated the same way and becomes a candidate with a verdict (`ck::EProceduralFootholdVerdict`):

- `Miss`: no trusted hit (a back face and an inside-solid origin are never trusted).
- `Unreachable`: farther than `_ForceStepReachFraction` of the reach from the hip. A down-ray hit beyond `_TargetReachFraction` is first clamped toward the hip and probed once more there, as the ideal target always was, except under a held foothold, which is judged at its own point (step 1); a face hit beyond it (the occluder's face, an inward or outward cast) is `Unreachable` at once, because a down ray there would find a different surface.
- `TooSteep`: for search candidates only, a normal more than `_Foothold._MaxAngle` from the support up. The ideal target and a held foothold accept any surface they do not see from behind, as the probe always did.
- `Inboard`: for search candidates only, a hit farther than a tenth of the reach inboard of the hip along the leg's own side (the direction from the hip to its rest foot in the support plane; `ck::Get_IsFootholdOnOwnSide`). A foothold there folds the leg under the body: a beam's side under the belly, a log's lower facet. The ideal target and a held foothold are not tested (the ideal is the rest foot plus the lead; a hold was a search candidate when it was chosen). A leg whose rest foot lies straight under its hip has no own side and is never `Inboard`. Gripping a surface under the body needs a pole that folds the leg inward, a rig question outside this module's scope; such a leg gathers at rest instead.
- `UnderBody`: for search candidates only, a hit closer to the body's centreline than its widest enabled hip less 5 cm (in the support frame, measured from the body) and deeper below its leg's hip than a quarter of the leg's rest drop (`ck::Get_IsFootholdUnderBody`). The own-side rule cannot see this for a leg whose rest direction runs along the body: a front or back leg of a crawler over a beam would otherwise hold the beam's side under the body's middle, and a beast would hold a log's lower facets under its belly. Such a leg folds under the body and its stretched hold re-steps beyond the schedule. The hips are taken to sit on the body's surface; hips inset from it make the rule slightly permissive, the safe side. The ideal target and a held foothold are not tested. A beam narrower than the hips is a course the search has no answer for: feet on its top would need a preference for the surface under the body, feet on its sides a rig whose pole folds the leg inward.
- `Occluded`: a line trace from the hip, with the probe's query filter, meets a solid farther than `_Foothold._OcclusionTolerance` from the hit. A trace that starts inside a solid (fraction 0, a hip inside the body's own collider) tells nothing and occludes nothing. The straight line is a proxy for the chain: a foot beyond an edge the chain would drape over reads occluded, and the search then offers the edge's face and the floor in front of it.
- `Usable` otherwise.

No verdict tests the swing itself: a foot may swing through a solid between its plant and its target, such as a pillar corner between a foot and the floor beyond it. Going around needs swing-path planning the gait does not have; a veto on targets whose swing meets a solid, with the plant held while nothing is usable, left feet stuck behind the solids the body walked past.

1. **Held foothold.** A leg that holds a foothold (`ck::FProceduralFootholdState` in `_Footholds`, in world space, never rotated with the support frame) re-validates it every solve, with one ray and the occlusion trace. The ray follows how the hold was found (`ck::EProceduralFootholdCast` on the state): a hold a down ray found (`Ring`, the `Front` floor) is checked with one down ray under it (the first attempt, the second only when the first starts inside a solid), and the reach and occlusion checks apply to that hit as it is: never clamped toward the hip and probed again like the ideal target, which would stand a different point in for the hold and, on continuous ground, never let a trailing hold read `Unreachable`; a hold a face cast found (the `Front` face, `Inward`, `Outward`) is checked with one ray along its own normal, from 10 cm in front of it to 10 cm behind, because a down ray under a point on a vertical face runs along the face and never meets it. A hold that is no longer usable is cleared.
2. **Ideal target.** The probe under the ideal target, three attempts as before, then the occlusion trace. A usable ideal is the target (`Ideal`) and clears the hold, unless a usable hold lies within the keep radius of it: then the hold stays the target (`Held`), so the foot does not hop between two spots that agree. An unusable ideal leaves a usable hold as the target while the two lie within `_ForceStepReachFraction` of the reach of each other (the leg's own usable radius): an ideal hanging beside a beam or over a gap would otherwise drop, on the next solve, the hold its own search found, and the leg would search, back off and gather into the air in turn. Farther apart, the body has walked away from the hold (up a log, over a crest) and a leg kept on it re-plants behind its rest point, so the hold is cleared and the search runs. The ideal target is never held, so where it always works no hold exists.
3. **Search**, only when neither the hold nor the ideal target is usable. Up to 13 candidates, in this order: when the ideal target was `Occluded`, two `Front` ones, the face the occlusion trace met and a down ray a tenth of the reach from it back toward the hip (the floor in front of the obstacle); two `Inward` casts from the ideal target toward `Hip − Up × 1.5 RestDrop` and `Hip − Up × 4 RestDrop`, extended by `_Probe._Down` (a pillar side facing the leg, the floor under a raised hip, a cylinder side curving away); one `Outward` cast from `Hip + Up × 2 RestDrop` through the ideal target, extended by `_Probe._Down` (a pillar top or face beyond it); eight `Ring` down rays on a circle of the search radius around the ideal target in the support plane (first attempt, second only from inside a solid). `RestDrop` is the leg's hip height over its rest foot. The casts centre on the leg's own hip, not the body's centre, so the end legs of a long body look under themselves rather than along it. The pure `ck::SelectProceduralFoothold` (`Core/CkProceduralFoothold.h`, no world) picks the least-cost `Usable` one of the search's own candidates in the support frame (a hold the force-step bound just cleared is not among them, so it is never picked again), `cost = |p − ideal| / reach + SlopeWeight × (1 − n·Up) + (planted ? ContinuityWeight × |p.z − plant.z| / reach : 0)`, ties to the earlier candidate. The pick is the target and becomes the leg's held foothold. A swing aimed at a searched or held foothold (the leg input's `_TargetIsFoothold`: any source but `Ideal` and `None`) gets neither the stroke overshoot nor the freeze push, so it lands on the spot the search validated. **Budget:** per update at most `max(4, enabled legs / 2)` legs run this step; the update visits the legs from the one after the last leg that searched (`_NextSearchLeg` on the gait fragment), so the searches go round. A leg past the budget, or one whose last search found nothing usable within the last three solves (`_SearchedSolve` on its foothold state), takes step 4 this solve instead. The held re-validation and the ideal probe are never budgeted, and setup searches every leg. Because a hold serves while the ideal target stays unusable, a failing leg searches once and then holds; the budget binds only while many legs fail together.
4. **Nothing usable.** The foot is untrusted and the contact grace runs as before, with one change: past its grace a leg gathers toward its rest target only when the ideal target was missed or out of reach. When the ideal target was occluded the plant holds, with no valid target, until a solve finds a foothold: a foot never swings toward a point its leg cannot reach without passing through a solid.
5. **Occluded plant.** Every planted, enabled leg traces from its hip to its plant each update, with the same filter and tolerance. An occluded plant is an Emergency that steps beyond the schedule (see **Reach and cadence**), so a foot the body carries behind a post steps free within about one step.
6. **Landing lift.** A swing onto a face is not lifted (see **Ground under the landing point**).

**Tunables.** `FCk_ProceduralGait_Foothold` (on the preset, in the tunables fragment and on the apply-preset request): `_SearchRadius` (cm; 0 derives 0.3 of the leg's reach), `_KeepRadius` (cm; 0 derives 1.5 step thresholds; how close a usable hold must lie to a usable ideal target to stay the target; while the ideal is unusable the bound is the force-step reach instead), `_MaxAngle` (90 degrees, in [0, 90], search candidates only), `_SlopeWeight` (0.5), `_ContinuityWeight` (0.25) and `_OcclusionTolerance` (3 cm); every field finite and non-negative. A radius of 0 meaning "derive" follows the timing's `_CadenceSpeedRef` and `_MaxSimultaneousSwings`.

**Ray budget.** A rig with a clearance policy adds its link rays (see **Chain clearance**). In steady state a leg casts its ideal probe (one ray unless an attempt starts inside a solid), the ideal target's occlusion trace and, while planted, the plant trace; a swinging leg adds its clearance and landing rays. So every enabled leg casts two traces more than before the search existed. A search costs up to 13 candidates, the down rays up to three attempts each plus a clamped re-probe, and one occlusion trace each: about 50 rays for one leg. A held leg also probes its ideal target every solve. The budget bounds a solve's searches to `max(4, enabled legs / 2)` legs, eight on a 16-leg walker, and the back-off keeps a leg whose search found nothing from repeating it on the next three solves; a hold on a face costs one ray, like one on a floor.

**Readouts.** The published foot's `_Foothold` (`ECk_ProceduralLeg_Foothold`, through `UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::Get_Foot`) names the source of this solve's target, and `UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::Get_IdealVerdict` the verdict of its ideal target. `UCk_Utils_ProceduralAnimation_Debug_UE::Get_RaysLastSolve(Gait)` (Blueprint and AngelScript; 0 for an invalid handle) returns the rays the gait's last setup or update cast, plus the link rays its rigs with a clearance policy cast that frame. The debug snapshot's leg keeps every candidate the solve validated (`_Footholds`: world position, normal, source and verdict), the chosen index, the source and whether the plant was occluded; its gait keeps the ray count.

## Scheduling and ownership

SurfaceMotion processes setup, steering and movement in `FGroup_Physics`, in that order: surface-motion setup, steering requests, update. A steering request enqueued on the `Add` frame lands after the setup seed, so the request wins. Its deferred root transform is settled by the ordinary transform pass. In `FGroup_Transform_Derived` the order is: leg requests (enable/disable, detach), gait setup and gait requests (apply preset), gait update, then body pose update and rig setup (both after the gait update), then rig update. The rig reads the offset the body pose computed this frame; the presentation entity's transform request is applied afterwards by the derived-transform settle barrier. Gait requests do not wait for setup: a preset applied before setup lands. The existing derived-transform settle barrier applies limb outputs before post-transform render consumers. Pending requests on a destroyed leg or gait complete `Failed_Cancelled` in `FGroup_EndPlay`.

No processor is parallel. Gait, SurfaceMotion and a rig with a clearance policy issue Jolt ray casts, and the Jolt query API is game-thread-only, so the rig update stays serial.

SurfaceMotion must be the sole movement owner when composed. Do not also integrate root velocity through another mover. Gait can instead consume a root driven by another system without SurfaceMotion. Its ground provider is CkJolt; purely Chaos collision that is absent from Jolt will not produce foot contacts.

All `Request_*` functions carry the Ck completion delegate. Invalid admission ensures, fires `Failed_NotEnqueued` and attaches no partial feature state. Request completion reports local processing, not network delivery.

## Core contract

`ck::FProceduralGaitSolver` and its swing/probe helpers contain no world or UObject operations. Coordinates use a support-aligned frame with +Z as up. This is a rotation about the world origin, not a moving body-local origin. Re-express stored state with `NewBasis.Inverse() * OldBasis`; sample translation velocity in world space before rotating it. Otherwise turning the support frame manufactures false velocity or moves planted feet.

All durations use FCk_Time; phase remains dimensionless. Explicit Reset establishes topology. Step rejects mismatched arrays, malformed settings and nonfinite inputs without partial mutation. Non-advancing evaluation uses a solver copy, preserving internal state. Core rejection results must be handled by adapters. Each leg input carries an enabled flag; disabled legs keep their index and are skipped by scheduling, take-off, landing and the airborne tuck. Each input also carries `_Hip` (support frame) and `_Reach` (centimetres); a reach of 0 disables the target clamp and the reach Emergency for that leg, so pure-core callers that set neither keep the reach-free behaviour. The priority rule applies regardless of reach. `_LandingGroundZ` (support-frame height, `-FLT_MAX` when unknown) is the ground under the swing's landing point; with a reach of 0 the swing lifts onto it without a reach check. `_PlantOccluded` marks a planted foot whose hip cannot see it; with a valid target it is an Emergency of ratio 1 that steps beyond the schedule. `_TargetIsFoothold` marks a target the caller validated as a spot to stand on; a swing aimed at it gets neither the stroke overshoot nor the freeze push, and once it is aimed at one before its freeze the overshoot stays off for the rest of that swing.

`ck::ComputeProceduralSwingPoint` (`Core/CkProceduralGaitSwingProfile.h`) returns the point the solver's swing from a start to a target passes at a swing phase, lifted along a given up by a given height: the swing settings' authored ease and arc where valid, else the solver's smooth-step ease and its parametric arc at the settings' apex phase and sharpness. The solver's sprint height scale, obstacle clearance and landing lift are not in it. The gait does not call it yet.

Foot probe states distinguish current hits from fallback targets. A brief miss withholds a target to hold the plant; after the duration-based grace expires, the foot may gather toward its fresh rest target. Guessed targets are never counted as trusted surface contacts. Retry spans are bounded and remove outward lean on fallback attempts, supporting both convex and concave geometry.

The ECS facade interprets MaxSimultaneousSwings = 0 as max(1, enabled leg count / 2); the pure core interprets zero as unlimited. Emergency and catch steps may exceed the nominal swing budget.

## Scope

This release targets rigid N-leg rigs with 1..8 segments per leg and static surfaces. It does not claim arbitrary surface pathfinding, moving-platform attachment, skeletal/ControlRig integration, combat sequencing, adding legs after the gait exists, or replicated/persisted simulation state. Gait pattern tables, settle and airborne tuning exist in the core but are not exposed on the data asset yet. The CkTests courses use authored patrol steering; their motion and articulation still run through production features.

The CkGameplayDebugger plugin provides the standalone Procedural Animation tool under Systems (`ck.ProceduralAnimationDebugger 1`) and an ECS inspector. `UCk_Utils_ProceduralAnimation_Debug_UE::Get_Snapshot` accepts the body, one of its legs or any other direct lifetime child of the body, and returns the body's accepted solver state (including the cadence speed reference the solver runs with, the reach cadence floor, 0 when no enabled leg bounds it, the count of enabled legs skipped by the floor, and the count of missed landing lifts), the surface motion's grounded, trusted-contact and contact-source state and the contact pending confirmation (its normal and how long it has been seen), actual probe results, each swinging leg's landing-ground probe and the landing point it was cast under, each leg's foothold candidates with their verdicts, the chosen one, its source and whether the plant was occluded, the rays of the last solve (with the rigs' link rays, overlaid when the snapshot is taken), per-leg enabled state and entity identity, each rigged leg's segment and foot transforms and its clearance policy, chain state, stored swivel and crossing-link count, and the body pose's status, offset and, when it conforms, its held and applied conform targets when the body has one. History contains copied values and releases live entity references on world/session teardown. The debugger does not rerun traces. Its preview draws the selected leg's foothold candidates as crosses tinted by verdict, a ring around the chosen one and the line from the hip to it, every leg's landing-ground probe and the surface motion's pending contact normal, and it tints a chain its rig reports `Crossing` in the error colour (the snapshot counts the crossing links without naming them, so the whole chain is tinted); its leg rows show the foothold source, the candidate count, an occluded plant and the rig's chain state with its swivel angle or crossing-link count, and its detail line the rays per solve. Its only simulation mutations are the leg actions on the selected leg, Disable/Enable and Detach, issued through `Request_EnableDisable` and `Request_Detach` like any gameplay caller.
