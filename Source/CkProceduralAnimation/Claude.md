# CkProceduralAnimation

Procedural N-leg locomotion with independent surface motion and rigid N-joint leg presentation. The public ECS entry points are `UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE`, `UCk_Utils_ProceduralGait_UE`, `UCk_Utils_ProceduralRig_UE`, `UCk_Utils_ProceduralBodyPose_UE`, `UCk_Utils_SurfaceMotion_UE` and the one-call accelerant `UCk_Utils_ProceduralAnimation_UE::Add_Walker`; their generated AngelScript namespaces are `utils_procedural_leg`, `utils_procedural_gait`, `utils_procedural_rig`, `utils_procedural_body_pose`, `utils_surface_motion` and `utils_procedural_animation`.

## Composition

The body is a lifetime-owned entity with a transform. Legs are child entities of the body (`FCk_Handle_ProceduralLeg`), connected through `FFragment_RecordOfProceduralLegs`. The order is fixed: legs, then gait, then rigs and the optional body pose.

1. **Legs.** `UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::Create(Body, FCk_ProceduralLeg_Spec)` once per leg. A leg carries a unique `FName` Id, a body-local placement (hip, rest foot, phase offset, step-threshold scale) and its chain geometry (1..8 hip-first segment lengths in centimetres plus a body-local pole). The rig poses the chain; the gait reads the sum of its segment lengths as the leg's reach. Creating a leg on a body that already has a gait is rejected; the topology is fixed for the gait's lifetime.
2. **Surface motion (optional).** `UCk_Utils_SurfaceMotion_UE::Add(Body, FCk_SurfaceMotion_Spec)` when this module owns locomotion. Like leg `Create`, gait `Add` and `Add_Walker`, it rejects a body pending destruction. The Spec splits into `_Contact` (clearance, probe reach, contact grace, query filter) and `_Movement` (max speed, surface turn rate, clearance speed, gravity). The accepted support frame is independent of the body's interpolated orientation. `Request_Steering` takes a world direction and speed; the direction is projected onto the accepted surface tangent.
3. **Gait.** `UCk_Utils_ProceduralGait_UE::Add(Body, GaitData)` requires 2..64 legs already connected. It captures the legs in record order; that order is the solver's leg index and never changes. `Get_Legs(Body)` returns the record's current order, which changes after a detach; address legs by `Get_Id` or keep the handles `Create` returned. Tuning comes from a `UCk_ProceduralGait_Data` preset (timing, step, probe), copied at `Add`. Every captured leg's rest foot must lie within the step's `_TargetReachFraction` of its reach from its hip; `Add`, `Request_ApplyPreset` and `Add_Walker` reject a layout or preset that breaks this atomically (see **Reach and cadence**). The gait consumes body motion and performs passive Jolt queries. It does not translate the body.
4. **Rigs.** `UCk_Utils_ProceduralRig_UE::Add(Leg, FCk_ProceduralRig_Spec)` binds caller-owned transform entities to one leg, hip-first, plus an optional foot. The segment count must equal the leg's segment-length count. Every part must carry a transform, be a unique direct lifetime child of the leg's body, and not be bound by another leg's rig. Segment geometry must be centred on its transform origin with length along local +X. Omit the foot with an empty handle; a stale supplied handle is an error. `_Solver` (`ECk_ProceduralRig_ChainSolver`) picks how the chain is posed: `Auto` aims a single segment from the hip toward the foot target at the segment's own length, uses two-bone IK for two segments and the curve for three or more; an explicit `Fabrik` or `Curve` applies to any chain of two or more, and `Add` rejects either on a single segment. The curve (`ck::SolveProceduralLegCurve`) lays the links hip-first along a planar cubic Bezier from hip to foot, bent toward the pole, and picks the arch height that ends the chain on the foot; FABRIK is seeded half along the pole, then straight at the target. The curve core rejects a bend along the hip-foot line instead of picking a side, so the rig then bends toward the body's up, then its right. A folded chain, whose foot comes within about 40 % of its length of the hip, can have no arch that closes on the foot (chains of few, uneven links open that gap earliest); the chain then ends off the foot. The reach clamp keeps gait targets short of full extension, where every solver straightens abruptly.
5. **Body pose (optional).** `UCk_Utils_ProceduralBodyPose_UE::Add(Gait, FCk_ProceduralBodyPose_Spec)` makes the drawn body sag and tilt toward the legs that no longer hold it up. The Spec names a caller-owned presentation entity (`_Presentation`: a live transform entity that is a direct lifetime child of the body, not the body itself and not a rig part) and receives `Offset * Body` every frame, where `Offset` is body-local; attach the body's visual there, not to the body. The target offset comes from the pure `ck::ComputeProceduralBodySupportPose` over the gait's captured legs: a leg supports unless it is disabled, detached or destroyed, the body drops `_Support._CollapseDrop` centimetres times the unsupported fraction of captured legs, and it tilts up to `_Support._MaxTilt` degrees about the horizontal axis perpendicular to the gap between the centroid of all captured hips and the centroid of the supporting hips, dipping the unsupported side. `Add` captures the hip of every captured leg into `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_SupportLayout`, so it requires every leg the gait captured to still be live and rejects a gait that has already lost one; a leg detached or destroyed later keeps its captured hip, so the body tilts toward it exactly as toward the same leg disabled. `Offset` follows the target through the engine's `UKismetMathLibrary::VectorSpringInterp` and `QuaternionSpringInterp` (`_Spring`: stiffness, critical damping factor, mass and `_MaxAttitudeLag`). `_Conform._Mode = PlantedFeet` also tilts and lifts the target toward the planted feet (see **Body conform**); the target the springs follow is the conform rotation times the support-loss rotation, at the support-loss drop plus the conform height. Every frame, before springing, the body pose splits the simulation body's rotation since the last frame into a swing (tilt) and a twist about the body's up, and carries the inverse swing into the offset, so the drawn body keeps its world tilt when the body's attitude steps (SurfaceMotion's rate-capped support rotation steps at every facet it crosses) and the springs then ease it to the new target; the yaw passes straight through. This applies with or without conform. `_Spring._MaxAttitudeLag` (default 22 degrees, `Add` rejects a negative value) limits only how much of each frame's swing is carried: all of it while the offset, without this frame's carry, trails its target by less than the max lag minus an 8-degree knee (the knee shrinks to the max lag when that is smaller), none of it at the max lag, a linear share between. A facet step is smoothed in full; on a sustained rotation such as a ramp-to-wall corner, which the critically damped spring alone would trail by twice the rate over its natural frequency (over 60 degrees through a 90-degree corner turned at SurfaceMotion's 240 degrees per second), the lag settles below the max lag and the drawn body's rate ramps up to the body's across the knee without a step. Nothing but the springs moves the offset toward its target, so a target that jumps (the support pose on losing legs, the conform fit refitting after it held) is eased like any other. 0 carries no swing: the drawn body turns with the body and the springs ease it. `Get_TargetOffset` returns the body-local target the springs followed on the last update (identity unless `Ready`). The simulation body is untouched: probes, the gait and SurfaceMotion never see the sag, so released debris cannot become ground. The rig composes hips and pole from `UCk_Utils_ProceduralBodyPose_UE::Get_Offset(Body) * Body`, so the legs stay attached to the drawn body while the feet stay where the gait put them; `Get_Offset` is identity unless the body pose is `Ready`, so a body without a pose, or with a failed one, keeps its hips on the body. Losing the presentation entity latches `ECk_ProceduralBodyPose_Failure::MissingPresentation`. The body pose keeps following the body after the gait latches a failure: its update excludes the gait's setup tag but not its failure fragment, because the presentation carries the body's visual. Lean from acceleration and impulses for hit reactions are not implemented.
6. **Readiness.** Gait, rig and surface motion each report `Get_Status() -> ECk_ProceduralAnimation_Status` (`PendingSetup`, `Ready`, `Failed`); wait for `Ready` on gait and rig. Body pose has no setup and reads `Ready` or `Failed`; `ProceduralBodyPose.Get_Offset` and `Get_TargetOffset` are identity unless it is `Ready`. A rig reads `PendingSetup` until its body's gait is `Ready`. Observe `Failed` rather than treating permanent failure as delayed readiness; `Get_Failure()` names the reason (`ECk_ProceduralGait_Failure`, `ECk_ProceduralRig_Failure`, `ECk_ProceduralBodyPose_Failure`, `ECk_SurfaceMotion_Failure`; `None` while not failed). An invalid handle or a handle without the feature reads `Failed`. Ready means set up, not grounded. `ProceduralGait.Get_PlantedCount` counts disabled legs as planted, because their frozen foot is planted. `SurfaceMotion.Get_Support` returns `Grounded` through the contact-grace interval; `Get_ContactQuery` reports whether the current query was `Trusted` or `Missed`. The reflected foot reports `ECk_ProceduralLeg_FootPhase` (`Planted`/`Swinging`) and `ECk_ProceduralLeg_FootContact` (`Trusted`/`Guessed`); `ProceduralLeg.Get_EnableDisable` reports the leg's `ECk_EnableDisable`. Gait, rig, body-pose and surface-motion failures latch: recovery requires replacing the composition. The rigid rig requires a unit-scale body; author segment lengths and part geometry at the intended size.

**Data assets.** `UCk_ProceduralGait_Data` is a tuning preset with no legs, reusable across creatures. `UCk_ProceduralRig_Data` is the leg layout for one creature: per-leg placement and chain geometry, no runtime handles. Both validate in the editor through `IsDataValid`. Values are copied into the runtime fragments at `Add`; editing the asset during play does not retune a live gait.

**Fragments.** Each feature's primary state fragment is its membership anchor: `Has` and `Cast` key on `ck::FFragment_ProceduralLeg`, `ck::FFragment_ProceduralGait`, `ck::FFragment_ProceduralRig` and `ck::FFragment_SurfaceMotion` alone. `Add` places `ck::FTag_ProceduralGait_NeedsSetup`, `ck::FTag_ProceduralRig_NeedsSetup` or `ck::FTag_SurfaceMotion_NeedsSetup`; the feature's `Setup` processor consumes it and every later processor excludes it. A latched failure is a data fragment (`ck::FFragment_ProceduralGait_Failure`, `ck::FFragment_ProceduralRig_Failure`, `ck::FFragment_SurfaceMotion_Failure`) whose presence excludes the entity from `Update` and whose field is the reason; readiness is derived from those two presences, never stored. SurfaceMotion splits steering intent (`ck::FFragment_SurfaceMotion`: direction and speed, the only data `Request_Steering` writes) from the accepted support frame (`ck::FFragment_SurfaceMotion_Support`: support normal, travel tangent, velocity, missing-contact time, support and contact-query enums, rewritten every substep). A disabled leg carries `ck::FFragment_ProceduralLeg_FrozenPose`. Body pose keeps the presentation, spring and support of its Spec in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_Params`, its body-local offset, the body-local target offset the springs last followed, the two engine spring states and the body rotation the offset was last carried to in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose` (the membership anchor), the captured legs' hips in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_SupportLayout`, a latched failure in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_Failure`, and, only when it conforms, the conform tunables in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_Conform` and the last fitted target in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_ConformState`; it places no setup tag because the engine springs initialise themselves on their first step. `_EvaluatedFrame` (SurfaceMotion) and `_PosedSolveSequence` (rig) record the frame and solve each feature last produced output for; the debugger reads them to judge freshness. Leg, rig and surface motion read every Spec field at steady state and never mutate it, so their config fragments alias the Spec (`FFragment_ProceduralLeg_Params = FCk_ProceduralLeg_Spec`, likewise for rig and surface motion). The gait's timing, step and probe live in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralGait_Tunables`, which `Request_ApplyPreset` replaces. `ck::FFragment_ProceduralGait_Debug` holds the last accepted snapshot and its per-leg scratch; only the gait update writes it and only `UCk_Utils_ProceduralAnimation_Debug_UE` reads it. The gait's `_SolveSequence` counts accepted solves: the rig stamps it and the snapshot copies it, so no production processor reads debug data. Solver inputs and outputs are per-tick locals of the gait update.

**Accelerant.** `UCk_Utils_ProceduralAnimation_UE::Add_Walker(Body, RigData, GaitData, Chains)` creates one leg per layout entry (asset order becomes record order), adds the gait, then binds each supplied `FCk_ProceduralWalker_LegChain` (leg Id + rig chain). It validates everything before the first mutation and returns an empty `FCk_ProceduralWalker` on rejection. An empty chain list yields a gait-only walker.

The module creates no render components or assets. Callers can use CkPmg or CkIsmRenderer for rigid parts; a future skeletal adapter can consume the same world foot outputs (`UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::Get_Foot`: position, normal, rotation, swing alpha, phase, contact).

## Disabling a leg

`UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::Request_EnableDisable` is reversible. A disabled leg leaves the step schedule and its foot freezes body-relative: the gait captures the foot pose in body space on the transition into `ck::FFragment_ProceduralLeg_FrozenPose`, present only while the leg is disabled, and re-expresses it every frame, so the rig keeps posing the leg and it rides rigidly with the body. Re-enabling removes the fragment. Disabled legs cost no Jolt queries. Survivors keep their indices; nothing is packed or re-indexed.

## Losing a leg

`UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::Request_Detach` is irreversible. In the leg's request drain it collects the parts the rig was posing, optionally transfers their lifetime ownership to the world (`ECk_ProceduralLeg_ReleasedPartsOwnership::TransferToWorld`), fires `OnProceduralLeg_Detached(Leg, ReleasedParts)` while the leg handle is still readable, then destroys the leg entity. The drain runs before the gait and rig updates, so released parts never receive another pose request. The gait masks the lost index and keeps running on the survivors.

Ownership stays with the body unless `TransferToWorld` is requested: destroying the body then destroys body-owned debris.

Ragdolling the released parts is the game's job. The recipe:

1. Bind `BindTo_OnDetached` on the leg, or read `UCk_Utils_ProceduralRig_UE::Get_Chain` first.
2. Call `Request_Detach`.
3. In the callback, for each released part: add a dynamic Jolt body with an explicit box shape sized like the part's visual (`FCk_JoltBody_Spec{ECk_JoltBody_ShapeSource::ExplicitShape}` with `FCk_Jolt_ShapeDimensions{ECk_Jolt_ShapeType::Box}` half-extents, `ECk_MotionType::Dynamic`, an explicit mass, then `UCk_Utils_JoltBody_UE::Add`). CkJolt does not read CkShapes. Give the body a collision profile the gait and surface-motion probes do not trace: the gym uses `Ragdoll` (PhysicsBody objects that ignore the Visibility channel the probes use). Released debris must never read as ground for the survivors.
4. Optionally push the parts with `UCk_Utils_JoltBody_UE::Request_AddImpulse` — only once `UCk_Utils_JoltBody_UE::Get_IsBodyAdded` is true, because an impulse before the body exists is dropped. The gym fixture (`CkProceduralAnimationGym_Fixture.as`) is the reference implementation of the Jolt body and the impulse. A CkTimer that destroys the debris later is an optional addition; the fixture does not add one.
5. Bind `UCk_Utils_ProceduralGait_UE::BindTo_OnLegSetChanged` to react to the new leg count: switch locomotion mode, or `Request_ApplyPreset` with a request built from a slower preset.

**Leg-loss policy.** `FCk_ProceduralGait_Timing::_LegLossPolicy` decides how survivors adapt. `KeepAuthoredOffsets` (default) keeps every leg's authored phase. `RedistributeOffsets` re-spaces the enabled legs evenly by index and blends there through the solver's pattern-blend machinery. With `_MaxSimultaneousSwings = 0` the swing budget is `max(1, enabled legs / 2)`, recomputed whenever the enabled set changes.

**Signals and live retune.** `OnProceduralGait_LegSetChanged(Gait, EnabledCount, TotalCount)` tracks the enabled set: it fires on the frame a leg is disabled, enabled, or detached or destroyed while enabled. Detaching or destroying an already-disabled leg leaves the enabled set unchanged and does not fire it. `TotalCount` is the number of legs the gait captured at `Add`, not the number still attached. `UCk_Utils_ProceduralGait_UE::Request_ApplyPreset` takes an `FCk_Request_ProceduralGait_ApplyPreset` (timing, step and probe; build it from a `UCk_ProceduralGait_Data` preset's `Get_Timing`, `Get_Step` and `Get_Probe`), copies those into the live gait and re-applies solver settings; leg state, planted feet and the gait clock are kept.

## Footfall events

A leg fires `OnProceduralLeg_Planted(Leg, Footfall)` when its published foot phase changes to `Planted` and `OnProceduralLeg_Lifted(Leg, Footfall)` when it changes to `Swinging`; bind with `UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::BindTo_OnPlanted` / `BindTo_OnLifted` (default policy `FireIfPayloadInFlightThisFrame`, `DoNothing`) and unbind with the `UnbindFrom_*` twins. Only the gait update fires them, right after it writes the leg's foot, so a plant and a lift alternate per leg and each change fires once. Gait setup seeds every foot as planted without firing. `FCk_ProceduralLeg_Footfall` carries the foot's world position and normal, its `ECk_ProceduralLeg_FootContact`, and `_LandingSpeed`: the foot's speed in cm/s over the frame it planted, 0 on a lift. There is no physical material: the Jolt hit carries none. Disabling a swinging leg freezes its foot as planted, and that transition fires a plant with a guessed contact; re-enabling fires nothing until the foot next lifts. A detached leg never fires: it is pending destruction from its request drain on, and the gait freezes its last pose without publishing a footfall.

## Body conform

`FCk_ProceduralBodyPose_Spec::_Conform` (`FCk_ProceduralBodyPose_Conform`: `_Mode`, `_MaxTilt` 20 degrees, `_HeightWeight` 0.5, `_MaxHeight` 10 cm) is unpacked at `Add`: `PlantedFeet` adds the conform fragments, `None` adds neither, and `Add` rejects a max tilt outside 0..89 degrees, a height weight outside 0..1 or a negative max height. Every frame the pure `ck::ComputeProceduralBodyConformPose` fits a weighted least-squares plane `h = a x + b y + c` through each captured foot's height above its rest height, at the foot's x and y in the simulation body's frame. A planted foot counts fully; a swinging foot fades out over the first third of its swing and back in over the last third, so the fit does not step when the planted set changes; a disabled, detached or destroyed leg counts zero. The fit tilts the target's up onto the plane's normal, clamped to `_MaxTilt`, with no turn about the body's up, and moves it `_HeightWeight` times `c` along the body's up, clamped to `_MaxHeight`. The core returns `Fitted`, `Underdetermined` (fewer than three weighted feet, or weighted feet along one line: the update holds the last fitted target in the body's frame) or `Malformed` (a non-finite foot or a negative weight: the update ensures and latches `ECk_ProceduralBodyPose_Failure::MalformedConform`). A two-group walker with four legs holds during every swing and refits while all four stand. The fit works in the body's frame, so it behaves the same on walls and ceilings, and the support-loss sag stays toward the surface the feet stand on; a game that wants gravity sag drives its own offset.

## Reach and cadence

A leg's reach is the sum of its segment lengths. `FCk_ProceduralGait_Step` carries two fractions of it, validated as `0 < _TargetReachFraction < _ForceStepReachFraction <= 1` (defaults 0.8 and 0.92); the solver holds them in `ck::FProceduralGaitReachSettings`.

- **Target clamp.** Every swing target stays within `_TargetReachFraction` of the hip: the take-off target (a catch step keeps its requested target, clamped), the damped retarget, the push at the retarget freeze phase, the target after the stroke overshoot (every frame, against the current hip), the swing a re-enabled leg begins, and the landing after airborne. `ck::FProceduralGaitSolver::ClampToReach` shrinks the planar offset in the support frame and keeps the height; a height alone beyond the limit clamps in 3D. Before probing, the gait clamps its query point (`Neutral + Lead`, with the velocity lead projected onto the support plane) the same way, so the probe looks for ground under a reachable point.
- **Ground within reach.** A probe hit farther than `_TargetReachFraction` of the reach from the hip is clamped the same way and probed once more there, so the target the solver receives is real ground within reach. The solver's clamp keeps a target's support-frame height, which across a convex bend (a crest, a ridge, a stair edge) lies inside the ground; the second probe keeps that clamp to trimming the stroke overshoot and the freeze push.
- **Unreachable ground holds the plant.** A final hit still farther than `_ForceStepReachFraction` of the reach from the hip is not trusted, like a miss: the plant holds through the contact grace, then gathers toward its rest target. The debug probe shows the final attempt. A foot never plants on ground far below a reachable point, as at a ledge or a gap. A hit whose normal faces along the ray (a back face) is never trusted either.
- **Reach Emergency.** A planted foot farther than `_ForceStepReachFraction` of the reach from its hip is an Emergency, joining the error Emergency (`Error > Threshold × EmergencyFactor`) with its rules: it needs a valid target, ignores the swing window and the budget, and waits for inhibition.
- **Priority.** The planted Emergency leg whose phase group has no swing in flight, with the largest ratio `max(Error / (Threshold × EmergencyFactor), |Plant − Hip| / (ForceStepFraction × Reach))`, holds back every non-emergency take-off in the other groups until it steps. Without it an Emergency leg starves: while a group swings, inhibition keeps it planted, and on the frame that group drains, leg index order hands the slot to another group. Emergency and catch steps of other groups are not held back and inhibition is unchanged, so take-offs never overlap two groups.
- **Cadence floor.** For each enabled leg, with `L` its reach, `dz` the hip's height over the rest foot and `lateral` the rest foot's body-Y offset from the hip: `R_t = sqrt(max(0, (TargetFraction × L)² − dz²))` and `R_f` likewise with `ForceStepFraction`. The leg strides along body X over the chord `U = sqrt(R_t² − lateral²) + sqrt(R_f² − lateral²)`, and is skipped when `lateral >= R_t`. With `Ts = CycleDuration − StepDuration`, the solver runs with `min(authored CadenceSpeedRef, 0.85 × min U / Ts)`, which keeps stance travel per cycle within 85 % of the shortest chord. The floor is rebuilt at `Add`, on `Request_ApplyPreset` and whenever the enabled set changes. An authored reference of 0 (cadence scaling off) stays 0; `Ts <= 0` or no striding leg leaves the authored value. Admission keeps every rest foot inside the target radius, so a skipped leg can only sit exactly on it. For the gym's walkers the floor is above the authored reference, so it changes nothing there; it guards layouts whose legs cannot cover the stance travel.

## Scheduling and ownership

SurfaceMotion processes setup, steering and movement in `FGroup_Physics`, in that order: surface-motion setup, steering requests, update. A steering request enqueued on the `Add` frame lands after the setup seed, so the request wins. Its deferred root transform is settled by the ordinary transform pass. In `FGroup_Transform_Derived` the order is: leg requests (enable/disable, detach), gait setup and gait requests (apply preset), gait update, then body pose update and rig setup (both after the gait update), then rig update. The rig reads the offset the body pose computed this frame; the presentation entity's transform request is applied afterwards by the derived-transform settle barrier. Gait requests do not wait for setup: a preset applied before setup lands. The existing derived-transform settle barrier applies limb outputs before post-transform render consumers. Pending requests on a destroyed leg or gait complete `Failed_Cancelled` in `FGroup_EndPlay`.

No processor is parallel. Gait and SurfaceMotion issue Jolt ray casts, and the Jolt query API is game-thread-only. The rig only reads its own leg and the body and writes through deferred transform requests, so it could become a parallel processor; that switch waits for a benchmark.

SurfaceMotion must be the sole movement owner when composed. Do not also integrate root velocity through another mover. Gait can instead consume a root driven by another system without SurfaceMotion. Its ground provider is CkJolt; purely Chaos collision that is absent from Jolt will not produce foot contacts.

All `Request_*` functions carry the Ck completion delegate. Invalid admission ensures, fires `Failed_NotEnqueued` and attaches no partial feature state. Request completion reports local processing, not network delivery.

## Core contract

`ck::FProceduralGaitSolver` and its swing/probe helpers contain no world or UObject operations. Coordinates use a support-aligned frame with +Z as up. This is a rotation about the world origin, not a moving body-local origin. Re-express stored state with `NewBasis.Inverse() * OldBasis`; sample translation velocity in world space before rotating it. Otherwise turning the support frame manufactures false velocity or moves planted feet.

All durations use FCk_Time; phase remains dimensionless. Explicit Reset establishes topology. Step rejects mismatched arrays, malformed settings and nonfinite inputs without partial mutation. Non-advancing evaluation uses a solver copy, preserving internal state. Core rejection results must be handled by adapters. Each leg input carries an enabled flag; disabled legs keep their index and are skipped by scheduling, take-off, landing and the airborne tuck. Each input also carries `_Hip` (support frame) and `_Reach` (centimetres); a reach of 0 disables the target clamp and the reach Emergency for that leg, so pure-core callers that set neither keep the reach-free behaviour. The priority rule applies regardless of reach.

Foot probe states distinguish current hits from fallback targets. A brief miss withholds a target to hold the plant; after the duration-based grace expires, the foot may gather toward its fresh rest target. Guessed targets are never counted as trusted surface contacts. Retry spans are bounded and remove outward lean on fallback attempts, supporting both convex and concave geometry.

The ECS facade interprets MaxSimultaneousSwings = 0 as max(1, enabled leg count / 2); the pure core interprets zero as unlimited. Emergency and catch steps may exceed the nominal swing budget.

## Scope

This release targets rigid N-leg rigs with 1..8 segments per leg and static surfaces. It does not claim arbitrary surface pathfinding, moving-platform attachment, skeletal/ControlRig integration, combat sequencing, adding legs after the gait exists, or replicated/persisted simulation state. Gait pattern tables, settle and airborne tuning exist in the core but are not exposed on the data asset yet. The CkTests courses use authored patrol steering; their motion and articulation still run through production features.

## Verification

Tests live in CkTests under `Private/UnitTests/CkProceduralAnimation/` and `Script/CkProceduralAnimation`. The registered gym is **Procedural Animation Surface Traversal** in the shared gym switchboard. It shows nine 4/6/8-leg rigs across uneven ground, ramp/wall and concave-loop courses. P controls travel, R resets, V toggles contact diagnostics, K disables or re-enables leg 0 of every crawler, J detaches leg 1 of the first crawler and ragdolls its parts (one-shot), Home frames the overview, 1/2/3 frame the courses. Each crawler's body is drawn on a body-pose presentation entity, so the crawler body sags toward disabled/detached legs (K/J). The 4-leg crawlers use a `RedistributeOffsets` preset, the 6- and 8-leg crawlers the default `KeepAuthoredOffsets`; a crawler that drops below three enabled legs applies the slow preset through `Request_ApplyPreset`. **N** cycles the same three course slots through four walker sets and resets them: the original crawlers above; a menagerie of a spider (8 legs x 4 segments), a centipede (16 x 2, stepping as a front-to-back wave), a tentacled walker (6 x 8, the rig's maximum) and a mixed-chain beast (2-segment front legs, 4-segment rear legs, trotting) on stairs, rubble and a convex hump; and two stress sets with three walkers each, a step field with 10 to 40 cm risers, a 150 cm ledge and a sharp 90-degree ridge, then a log of radius 80 cm, a 30 cm beam per walker and a field of 40 cm pillars. The PaViz harness also spins walkers in place; the gym does not. Every walker in both sets is composed through `Add_Walker` from rig and gait data assets plus a body pose that conforms to its planted feet; the 6-leg crawler sets its chains to `Fabrik` and the tentacled walker to `Curve`, the rest use `Auto`. K, J and 1/2/3 act on whichever set is live. Source presence and UHT generation do not prove runtime traversal or visual quality.

## Debugger

The CkGameplayDebugger plugin provides the standalone Procedural Animation tool under Systems (`ck.ProceduralAnimationDebugger 1`) and an ECS inspector. `UCk_Utils_ProceduralAnimation_Debug_UE::Get_Snapshot` accepts the body, one of its legs or any other direct lifetime child of the body, and returns the body's accepted solver state (including the cadence speed reference the solver runs with, the reach cadence floor, 0 when no enabled leg bounds it, and the count of enabled legs skipped by the floor), actual probe results, per-leg enabled state and entity identity, each rigged leg's segment and foot transforms, and the body pose's status, offset and, when it conforms, its held conform target when the body has one. History contains copied values and releases live entity references on world/session teardown. The debugger does not rerun traces. Its only simulation mutations are the leg actions on the selected leg, Disable/Enable and Detach, issued through `Request_EnableDisable` and `Request_Detach` like any gameplay caller.
