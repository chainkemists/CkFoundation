# CkProceduralAnimation

Procedural N-leg locomotion with independent surface motion and rigid N-joint leg presentation. The public ECS entry points are `UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE`, `UCk_Utils_ProceduralGait_UE`, `UCk_Utils_ProceduralRig_UE`, `UCk_Utils_ProceduralBodyPose_UE`, `UCk_Utils_SurfaceMotion_UE` and the one-call accelerant `UCk_Utils_ProceduralAnimation_UE::Add_Walker`; their generated AngelScript namespaces are `utils_procedural_leg`, `utils_procedural_gait`, `utils_procedural_rig`, `utils_procedural_body_pose`, `utils_surface_motion` and `utils_procedural_animation`.

## Composition

The body is a lifetime-owned entity with a transform. Legs are child entities of the body (`FCk_Handle_ProceduralLeg`), connected through `FFragment_RecordOfProceduralLegs`. The order is fixed: legs, then gait, then rigs and the optional body pose.

1. **Legs.** `UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::Create(Body, FCk_ProceduralLeg_Spec)` once per leg. A leg carries a unique `FName` Id, a body-local placement (hip, rest foot, phase offset, step-threshold scale) and its chain geometry (1..8 hip-first segment lengths in centimetres plus a body-local pole). The rig poses the chain; the gait reads the sum of its segment lengths as the leg's reach. Creating a leg on a body that already has a gait is rejected; the topology is fixed for the gait's lifetime. Optional `_FootContactRadius` is a finite non-negative centimetre radius for contact reservations; 0 disables contact reservations and is independent of rig `_SegmentClearanceRadii`.
2. **Surface motion (optional).** `UCk_Utils_SurfaceMotion_UE::Add(Body, FCk_SurfaceMotion_Spec)` when this module owns locomotion. Like leg `Create`, gait `Add` and `Add_Walker`, it rejects a body pending destruction. The Spec splits into `_Contact` (clearance, probe reach, contact grace, confirm angle 30 degrees, confirm time 0.075 s, height source, max step height 0, wall policy `Climb`, query filter) and `_Movement` (max speed, surface turn rate, clearance speed, gravity, steer floor 0.4); `Add` also rejects a confirm angle outside 0..180 degrees, a negative or non-finite confirm time, a steer floor outside 0..1 and a max step height that is neither 0 nor above the clearance and at most the probe reach. The accepted support frame is independent of the body's interpolated orientation. `Request_Steering` takes a world direction and speed; the direction is projected onto the accepted surface tangent, unless that projection is shorter than the steer floor's share of the direction: on a face nearly perpendicular to the steering (a log's lower facets, a wall under a horizontal route) the projection is a short vector whose direction flips from facet to facet, so the body keeps its travel tangent laid onto the new plane instead. Each update runs 16 ms substeps through the pure `ck::StepProceduralSurfaceMotionPaced` (`Core/CkProceduralSurfaceMotion.h`), which replays the surface step through a Jolt ray adapter when a trusted planted stance constrains motion:
   - **Rays.** Forward from the body within the clearance, at the body's height, so a face lower than the clearance is never seen by the forward ray: the down ray lifts the body over it once it is under the body; a face the forward ray does meet is a step or a wall (see **Steps and walls**). Down from one clearance above the body for the probe reach; a look-ahead down ray from 1.5 clearances ahead, which counts only on a surface within 15 degrees of the support normal; and, around a convex edge, a fan back and down from one clearance ahead. The forward hit wins, then the down hit. The look-ahead replaces a down hit that lies below the clearance when its own hit lies more than a quarter clearance higher: after the fan turns the body onto a ledge or beam top one clearance behind the edge, the down ray sees the lower floor, and following it would sink the body back below the edge. After a down miss the look-ahead comes before the fan. The forward ray and the fan are cast while steering speed is positive, including a paced substep with zero voluntary travel; the down ray and the look-ahead run at any speed, so a body that stops within a clearance past a crest keeps the top it stands at instead of sinking to the floor below or falling. Every contact about to be adopted needs a clearance of room off it (see **Steps and walls**). `Get_ContactSource` reports which ray the body's last accepted contact came from (`ECk_SurfaceMotion_ContactSource`: `None` while nothing supports the body, `Forward`, `Down`, `LookAhead`, `Fan`, `Fall`, `Feet` for the plane through the planted feet, and `Step` for the top of a face the body steps onto).
   - **Look-ahead contact localization.** A remote tangent-plane contact can hold a body away from a curved surface after its own down ray misses. `DoLocalize_LookAhead` (`Core/CkProceduralSurfaceMotion.cpp`) casts one additional ray from the current candidate position toward the observed ahead hit displaced inward by one clearance along that hit normal; the ray length is capped at `_Contact._ProbeReach`. A current local hit replaces the complete ahead contact only when its normal has positive alignment with the ahead normal and the body has room on it. Both its actual hit position and actual hit normal are used together for height and contact adoption. A miss, a perpendicular/back-facing hit or insufficient room retains the complete original ahead contact, preserving the raised top across a crest rather than combining a side position with the top normal. This bounded refinement is shared by look-ahead selection over a lower down hit and after a down miss; existing normal confirmation and adoption still apply.
   - **Planted reach pace.** After each successful gait solve, the gait publishes its trusted planted feet, hip-local positions, reaches and source body transform. SurfaceMotion uses only a matching prior solve whose legs are still live, enabled and trusted at those positions. Each substep checks the requested body motion against every chain, then replays at zero and up to six bounded partial scales when needed; only a checked body and contact state pair is committed. Simulation hips and the current ready body-pose offset hips have separate reach limits. The zero-scale trial still queries contacts, confirms surfaces, resolves obstruction and falls. `Get_ReachPaceScale` reads the lowest accepted voluntary scale in the update; `Get_ReachPaceState` distinguishes `Free`, `Pacing`, `Blocked` and `PhysicalOverride` (the zero-scale trial itself exceeded a prior chain limit). A blocked walker continues contact and gait probes but needs a usable foothold or new steering to advance. A changed body or leg since the gait solve invalidates its old anchors. The later body-pose spring step checks the fresh stance again before publishing drawn hips; the pace state alone still reports only the earlier Physics decision.
   - **Paced cadence.** A rejected full-scale reach trial and its zero-voluntary trial also measure how far the constrained simulation or ready presentation hips would have moved; SurfaceMotion aggregates that attempted stance distance over the frame. A fresh, same-body, grounded Pacing or Blocked sample may drive the gait clock and cadence scale from that speed while the body waits for feet to replant, including a turn in place. Authored pattern selection and at-rest settling continue to use measured body speed; target prediction continues to use measured planar velocity. Free, airborne, physically overridden and stale motion samples use the original measured cadence.
   - **Reach-limited plant release.** The final physics substep may publish the rejected full voluntary trial and its offending trusted plant identities, without extra traces. Gait consumes it only for the same evaluated frame and accepted body, a live enabled leg with the same owner, and the unchanged trusted plant. The selected trusted replacement must fit both simulation and ready presentation hips in that very trial while the old plant does not. A qualifying leg can use the existing budgeted, single-group beyond-schedule path and contact reservations; no plant is moved or fabricated. An inactive constrained phase reserves its actual limiting leg before ordinary error priority can spend the slot. A constrained leg joining an active scheduled phase must meet the existing bounded join deadline, otherwise its relief is explicitly beyond schedule and cannot extend that scheduled group. Physical override, zero time, absent/stale trials, invalid or equally restrictive replacements grant no relief. Existing ordinary/catch emergency policies are unchanged; this does not guarantee uninterrupted motion when capacity or a trusted relieving foothold is unavailable.
   - **Height source.** `_Contact._HeightSource` (`ECk_SurfaceMotion_HeightSource`, default `Rays`) decides what holds the body up: `Rays` keeps it at its clearance above what its rays hit; `PlantedFeet` also lets it ride the plane its gait fits through the ground its feet stand on and are about to land on (see **Body height from the planted feet**). `Get_HeightSource` reads it back. A body without a gait has no such plane and keeps to its rays.
   - **Confirmation.** A contact whose normal turns more than the confirm angle from the support while the down ray still hits is adopted only once it has been seen for the confirm time, each sighting within 15 degrees of the last. Until then the body keeps the support under it, or coasts along its plane when the down ray itself proposed the turn, and `Get_ContactQuery` stays `Trusted`; a coasting substep adopts nothing, so `Get_ContactSource` keeps the source it last accepted. A face the rays graze for a few substeps (a rock side under a tilted down ray, a riser top edge at clearance height) is never adopted. Without a planted-stance pace constraint, the body continues at its requested speed while it waits, so a body longer than its clearance can overrun a head-on wall by up to its speed times the confirm time (13.5 cm at 180 cm/s) before it turns. Pacing may reduce or pause voluntary travel while contact confirmation advances in real time. A down ray that hits nothing adopts any contact at once, so convex edges turn as soon as the fan finds the next face; a face the down ray meets along its length but does not trust (grazed along the seam two solids share, or facing away) still counts as support for the confirmation, so a seam cannot fast-track a large turn, while a down ray that starts on or inside a solid tells nothing about the ground below and counts as a miss.
   - **Contact loss.** Without a trusted hit the body coasts for the contact grace, then falls under gravity along a swept ray. The landing keeps the heading the body travelled with: its travel tangent laid onto the landing plane, then the steering, then its own forward.
   - `Get_Velocity` is the last substep's velocity.
3. **Gait.** `UCk_Utils_ProceduralGait_UE::Add(Body, GaitData)` requires 2..64 legs already connected. It captures the legs in record order; that order is the solver's leg index and never changes. `Get_Legs(Body)` returns the record's current order, which changes after a detach; address legs by `Get_Id` or keep the handles `Create` returned. Tuning comes from a `UCk_ProceduralGait_Data` preset (timing, step, probe, foothold), copied at `Add`; `Add`, `Request_ApplyPreset` and `Add_Walker` reject a preset any of whose four parts is malformed. Every captured leg's rest foot must lie within the step's `_TargetReachFraction` of its reach from its hip; `Add`, `Request_ApplyPreset` and `Add_Walker` reject a layout or preset that breaks this atomically (see **Reach and cadence**). The gait consumes body motion and performs passive Jolt queries. It does not translate the body.
4. **Rigs.** `UCk_Utils_ProceduralRig_UE::Add(Leg, FCk_ProceduralRig_Spec)` binds caller-owned transform entities to one leg, hip-first, plus an optional foot. The segment count must equal the leg's segment-length count. Every part must carry a transform, be a unique direct lifetime child of the leg's body, and not be bound by another leg's rig. Segment geometry must be centred on its transform origin with length along local +X. Omit the foot with an empty handle; a stale supplied handle is an error. `_Solver` (`ECk_ProceduralRig_ChainSolver`) picks how the chain is posed: `Auto` aims a single segment from the hip toward the foot target at the segment's own length, uses two-bone IK for two segments and the curve for three or more; an explicit `Fabrik` or `Curve` applies to any chain of two or more, and `Add` rejects either on a single segment. The curve (`ck::SolveProceduralLegCurve`) lays the links hip-first along a planar cubic Bezier from hip to foot, bent toward the pole, and picks the arch height that ends the chain on the foot; FABRIK is seeded half along the pole, then straight at the target. The curve core rejects a bend along the hip-foot line instead of picking a side, so the rig then bends toward the body's up, then its right. For folded chains where the sampled Bezier branch leaves an endpoint gap, a rigid-link closure first keeps the curve's proximal link and solves its suffix for at most 64 iterations; if that cannot close to 0.01 cm, it retries the full chain for at most 64 more iterations, retaining only the best-improving pose. Coincident hip and foot, targets inside the chain's inner reach, and branch discontinuities can still leave a gap; the closure does not guarantee exact contact. The reach clamp keeps gait targets short of full extension, where every solver straightens abruptly. `_Clearance` (`ECk_ProceduralRig_Clearance`, default `None`) opts the rig into keeping its posed chain out of solids by swivelling the knee (see **Chain clearance**). Optional `_SegmentClearanceRadii` supplies one finite, non-negative centimetre radius per segment for sibling-chain capsules; an empty array preserves legacy posing and excludes that chain from sibling testing.
5. **Body pose (optional).** `UCk_Utils_ProceduralBodyPose_UE::Add(Gait, FCk_ProceduralBodyPose_Spec)` makes the drawn body sag and tilt toward the legs that no longer hold it up. The Spec names a caller-owned presentation entity (`_Presentation`: a live transform entity that is a direct lifetime child of the body, not the body itself and not a rig part) and receives `Offset * Body` every frame, where `Offset` is body-local; attach the body's visual there, not to the body. The target offset comes from the pure `ck::ComputeProceduralBodySupportPose` over the gait's captured legs: a leg supports unless it is disabled, detached or destroyed, the body drops `_Support._CollapseDrop` centimetres times the unsupported fraction of captured legs, and it tilts up to `_Support._MaxTilt` degrees about the horizontal axis perpendicular to the gap between the centroid of all captured hips and the centroid of the supporting hips, dipping the unsupported side. `Add` captures the hip of every captured leg into `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_SupportLayout`, so it requires every leg the gait captured to still be live and rejects a gait that has already lost one; a leg detached or destroyed later keeps its captured hip, so the body tilts toward it exactly as toward the same leg disabled. `Offset` follows the target through the engine's `UKismetMathLibrary::VectorSpringInterp` and `QuaternionSpringInterp` (`_Spring`: stiffness, critical damping factor, mass and `_MaxAttitudeLag`). `_Conform._Mode = PlantedFeet` also tilts and lifts the target toward the planted feet (see **Body conform**); the target the springs follow is the conform rotation times the support-loss rotation, at the support-loss drop plus the conform height. Every frame, before springing, the body pose splits the simulation body's rotation since the last frame into a swing (tilt) and a twist about the body's up, and carries the inverse swing into the offset, so the drawn body keeps its world tilt when the body's attitude steps (SurfaceMotion's rate-capped support rotation steps at every facet it crosses) and the springs then ease it to the new target; the yaw passes straight through. This applies with or without conform. `_Spring._MaxAttitudeLag` (default 22 degrees, `Add` rejects a negative value) limits only how much of each frame's swing is carried: all of it while the offset, without this frame's carry, trails its target by less than the max lag minus an 8-degree knee (the knee shrinks to the max lag when that is smaller), none of it at the max lag, a linear share between. A facet step is smoothed in full; on a sustained rotation such as a ramp-to-wall corner, which the critically damped spring alone would trail by twice the rate over its natural frequency (over 60 degrees through a 90-degree corner turned at SurfaceMotion's 240 degrees per second), the lag settles below the max lag and the drawn body's rate ramps up to the body's across the knee without a step. Without a reach constraint, only the springs move the offset toward its target, so a target that jumps (the support pose on losing legs) is eased like any other; the conform part of the target is rate-limited before the springs see it (see **Body conform**). 0 carries no swing: the drawn body turns with the body and the springs ease it. `Get_TargetOffset` returns the body-local target the springs followed on the last update, with the conform part slewed (identity unless `Ready`). The simulation body is untouched: probes and gait targets use it, while reach-aware touchdown and SurfaceMotion pacing also check ready posed hips. After springing, BodyPose projects the offset from its previous feasible pose toward the spring result whenever a fresh gait sample with trusted plants or published swinging feet would put a drawn hip beyond max(chain reach, simulation-hip distance); if the previous offset was already outside that bound, it starts from the simulation-aligned identity offset, which can visibly recover in one step. The gait uses the current ready posed hip as an additional planted-reach urgency signal, allowing that foot to replant before visual reach blocks the body; it still uses the simulation hip for foothold queries, target validity and reach clamping. It keeps the authored target and scales spring velocity by the accepted pose fraction, so a blocked offset does not build velocity through the chain. With no trusted plants or swinging feet, the spring output is unchanged. Released debris cannot become ground. The rig composes hips and pole from `UCk_Utils_ProceduralBodyPose_UE::Get_Offset(Body) * Body`, so the legs stay attached to the drawn body while the feet stay where the gait put them; `Get_Offset` is identity unless the body pose is `Ready`, so a body without a pose, or with a failed one, keeps its hips on the body. Losing the presentation entity latches `ECk_ProceduralBodyPose_Failure::MissingPresentation`. The body pose keeps following the body after the gait latches a failure: its update excludes the gait's setup tag but not its failure fragment, because the presentation carries the body's visual. Lean from acceleration and impulses for hit reactions are not implemented.
6. **Readiness.** Gait, rig and surface motion each report `Get_Status() -> ECk_ProceduralAnimation_Status` (`PendingSetup`, `Ready`, `Failed`); wait for `Ready` on gait and rig. Body pose has no setup and reads `Ready` or `Failed`; `ProceduralBodyPose.Get_Offset` and `Get_TargetOffset` are identity unless it is `Ready`. A rig reads `PendingSetup` until its body's gait is `Ready`. Observe `Failed` rather than treating permanent failure as delayed readiness; `Get_Failure()` names the reason (`ECk_ProceduralGait_Failure`, `ECk_ProceduralRig_Failure`, `ECk_ProceduralBodyPose_Failure`, `ECk_SurfaceMotion_Failure`; `None` while not failed). An invalid handle or a handle without the feature reads `Failed`. Ready means set up, not grounded. `ProceduralGait.Get_PlantedCount` counts disabled legs as planted, because their frozen foot is planted. `SurfaceMotion.Get_Support` returns `Grounded` through the contact-grace interval; `Get_ContactQuery` reports whether the current query was `Trusted` or `Missed`, and `Get_ContactSource` which ray it came from. The reflected foot reports `ECk_ProceduralLeg_FootPhase` (`Planted`/`Swinging`), `ECk_ProceduralLeg_FootContact` (`Trusted`/`Guessed`: while planted, whether the foot touched down on ground, kept until it next touches down: the gait confirms every touchdown with one short ray through the plant along the normal of the ground the swing aimed at, and the foot is `Trusted` when that ray meets ground, which also places the planted foot on the hit and gives it the hit's normal; while swinging, whether this solve's target is ground its gait probed and trusts) and `ECk_ProceduralLeg_Foothold`, where this solve's target came from (`Ideal`, `Held`, or a search source `Front`, `Inward`, `Outward`, `Ring`; `None` when nothing was usable, and on a disabled leg; see **Foothold search**); `ProceduralLeg.Get_IdealVerdict` reports why that solve's ideal target was or was not usable (`ECk_ProceduralLeg_FootholdVerdict`, the reflected mirror of the gait's verdict; a disabled leg keeps its last one); `ProceduralLeg.Get_EnableDisable` reports the leg's `ECk_EnableDisable`. Gait, rig, body-pose and surface-motion failures latch: recovery requires replacing the composition. The rigid rig requires a unit-scale body; author segment lengths and part geometry at the intended size.

**Data assets.** `UCk_ProceduralGait_Data` is a tuning preset with no legs, reusable across creatures. `UCk_ProceduralRig_Data` is the leg layout for one creature: per-leg placement and chain geometry, no runtime handles. Both validate in the editor through `IsDataValid`. Values are copied into the runtime fragments at `Add`; editing the asset during play does not retune a live gait.

**Fragments.** Each feature's primary state fragment is its membership anchor: `Has` and `Cast` key on `ck::FFragment_ProceduralLeg`, `ck::FFragment_ProceduralGait`, `ck::FFragment_ProceduralRig` and `ck::FFragment_SurfaceMotion` alone. `Add` places `ck::FTag_ProceduralGait_NeedsSetup`, `ck::FTag_ProceduralRig_NeedsSetup` or `ck::FTag_SurfaceMotion_NeedsSetup`; the feature's `Setup` processor consumes it and every later processor excludes it. A latched failure is a data fragment (`ck::FFragment_ProceduralGait_Failure`, `ck::FFragment_ProceduralRig_Failure`, `ck::FFragment_SurfaceMotion_Failure`) whose presence excludes the entity from `Update` and whose field is the reason; readiness is derived from those two presences, never stored. SurfaceMotion splits steering intent (`ck::FFragment_SurfaceMotion`: direction and speed, the only data `Request_Steering` writes) from the accepted support frame (`ck::FFragment_SurfaceMotion_Support`, which holds the core's `ck::FProceduralSurfaceMotionState`: support normal, travel tangent, velocity, missing-contact time, grounded and trusted-contact flags, contact source, the contact pending confirmation and the obstruction the substep slid along, rewritten every substep). `ck::FFragment_ProceduralLeg` holds the published foot and the ideal target's verdict, both written by the gait. `ck::FFragment_ProceduralGait_FeetPlane` holds the plane the gait's last update fitted through the ground its feet stand on and are about to land on, as the `ck::FProceduralSurfaceFeetSupport` SurfaceMotion rides (world point and normal, the frame it was fitted in, the footprint of the ground points that weigh anything), its state (`ck::EProceduralGaitFeetPlane`: `None`, `Fitted`, `Held`) and the time since the last fit; the gait's `Add` places it, only the gait update writes it and SurfaceMotion's update reads it. A disabled leg carries `ck::FFragment_ProceduralLeg_FrozenPose`. Body pose keeps the presentation, spring and support of its Spec in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_Params`, its body-local offset, the body-local target offset the springs last followed, the two engine spring states and the body rotation the offset was last carried to in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose` (the membership anchor), the captured legs' hips in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_SupportLayout`, a latched failure in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_Failure`, and, only when it conforms, the conform tunables in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_Conform` and, in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralBodyPose_ConformState`, the last fitted target and the applied target the springs chase; it places no setup tag because the engine springs initialise themselves on their first step. `_EvaluatedFrame` (SurfaceMotion) and `_PosedSolveSequence` (rig) record the frame and solve each feature last produced output for; the debugger reads them to judge freshness. `ck::FFragment_ProceduralRig` also keeps the posed joints and, for a rig with a clearance policy, the stored swivel angle, the chain state and the crossing-link count of its last pose. Leg, rig and surface motion read every Spec field at steady state and never mutate it, so their config fragments alias the Spec (`FFragment_ProceduralLeg_Params = FCk_ProceduralLeg_Spec`, likewise for rig and surface motion). The gait's timing, step, probe and foothold live in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralGait_Tunables`, which `Request_ApplyPreset` replaces. `ck::FFragment_ProceduralGait::_Footholds` holds each leg's held foothold (`ck::FProceduralFootholdState`: world position, normal, source, whether one is held, how it was found and the solve of its last search that found nothing better), sized with `_Probes` at `Add`; `_NextSearchLeg` is the leg the next update's search budget starts from. `ck::FFragment_ProceduralGait_Debug` holds the last accepted snapshot, its per-leg scratch, the reach cadence floor and skipped-leg count of the last solver-settings rebuild, and `_RaysLastSolve`, the rays the last setup or update cast, to which the rigs with a clearance policy add their link rays after the update; the gait's `Add`, its setup, its request drain and its update write it, the rig update adds to its ray count, and only `UCk_Utils_ProceduralAnimation_Debug_UE` reads it. The gait's `_SolveSequence` counts accepted solves: the rig stamps it and the snapshot copies it, so no production processor reads debug data. Solver inputs and outputs are per-tick locals of the gait update. Plain `ck::` types that are neither fragments nor tags, such as `FProceduralGaitBuiltSettings`, the solver-settings build result, live in the feature's `_Fragment_Data.h` with the reflected types.

**Accelerant.** `UCk_Utils_ProceduralAnimation_UE::Add_Walker(Body, RigData, GaitData, Chains)` creates one leg per layout entry (asset order becomes record order), adds the gait, then binds each supplied `FCk_ProceduralWalker_LegChain` (leg Id + rig chain). It validates everything before the first mutation and returns an empty `FCk_ProceduralWalker` on rejection. An empty chain list yields a gait-only walker.

The module creates no render components or assets. Callers can use CkPmg or CkIsmRenderer for rigid parts; a future skeletal adapter can consume the same world foot outputs (`UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::Get_Foot`: position, normal, rotation, swing alpha, phase, contact, foothold source).

## Chain clearance

`FCk_ProceduralRig_Spec::_Clearance` decides whether the rig keeps its posed chain out of solids and out of its drawn body. `None`, the default, poses the chain toward the leg's authored pole and casts no ray. `Swivel`:

- **Link test.** After the chain is posed, by any solver, each link (joint i to joint i + 1, from the hip to the foot) is tested with one ray using the body gait's `_Probe._QueryFilter`; the rig reads the gait's tunables on its body, which always has a gait. A hit with fraction in (0.02, 0.98) crosses a solid; a fraction-0 hit crosses too, because the link starts inside one; a hit within the last 2 % is the surface the next joint stands on and does not, and neither does one within the first 2 %, a joint resting on the surface its link leaves.
- **Body slab.** The link rays see only solids, and a drawn body may have no collider, so the rig also keeps the chain's interior joints (every joint but the hip and the foot) out of a slab standing in for the body: the box of the hips of every leg the gait captured, in the drawn body's frame (the body pose's offset times the body), 5 cm wider in X and Y and a quarter of the posed leg's rest drop above and below the hips. A link with an interior joint inside the slab counts as crossing. The slab casts no ray.
- **Swivel fan.** When a link crosses, the chain is posed again with the pole rotated about the hip-to-foot line by each angle of `ck::ProceduralPoleSwivelFanDegrees` (0, 30, -30, 60, -60, 90, -90, 120, -120, 150, -150; the wide tiers last, so they are tried only when every narrower angle crosses), in the order `ck::Get_ProceduralPoleSwivelOrder` gives: the leg's last clear angle first, then 0, then the fan's other angles in fan order. Each candidate is tested the same way. The first pose clear of solids and of the slab is kept and its angle stored; when none is, the pose with the fewest crossing links is kept (ties to the smaller absolute angle) and the stored angle becomes 0. There the authored pole (angle 0) counts only the links its rays find crossing, so a rig with the policy never poses worse than one without. The pure `ck::ComputeProceduralPoleSwivel` (`Core/CkProceduralChainClearance.h`, no world) rotates the pole right-handed about hip->foot. Every solver receives the swivelled pole exactly as it receives the authored one: the aim ignores it, two-bone IK takes it as its joint target, FABRIK seeds half the chain along it, the curve bends toward it; the segments' roll follows it too. The foot never moves: the swivel changes the knee, not the target.
- **Authored bend admission.** Before world tests or sibling scoring, every non-aim swivel candidate must preserve the authored pole's side in the posed body tangent plane and the actual first interior joint's authored tangent side and bend hemisphere about hip-to-foot. The reference joint is solved once at angle 0; an already-negative authored signed distance remains the lower bound, so authored geometry is preserved rather than silently corrected. A degenerate projected pole bend uses the actual authored joint's bend; a pure-up pole declares no tangent-side preference. The same admission applies to cached angles, coarse angles and refined sibling alternatives. It may retain an authored world-crossing pose when every clear alternative inverts the leg. Neither foot targets nor body motion change.
- **Continuity.** The stored angle is tried first on the next solve, so an admitted knee pose that went round a pillar stays there while the pillar is in the way and returns to 0 once its angle stops being clear and 0 is (0 is always tried second). The angle is not blended: the knee jumps between fan angles, and a 30-degree jump is visible at 60 Hz. Blending the angle is the recorded follow-up.
- **Limits.** A link whose far end lies inside a solid, such as a swinging foot passing through a pillar, crosses at every angle; so does a chain whose only clear bends lie more than 150 degrees from the authored pole. Both keep the pose with the fewest crossing links and read `Crossing`. The link test sees only what the probe's filter sees in the Jolt world, and the slab is only a box through the hips: a drawn body that reaches beyond its hips is covered only as far as the margins, and the authored pole's pose is not slab-tested once nothing clears.
- **Cost.** With empty sibling radii, per rigged leg per frame: one ray per link while the stored angle stays clear, up to eleven per link while the chain crosses. The authored reference adds one pure chain solve, reused when testing angle 0, without another raycast. The rays count toward the gait's `Get_RaysLastSolve`, which the rig adds to after the gait update in the same frame.
- **Failure and readouts.** A chain no angle clears is a diagnostic, not a failure: no failure reason exists for it. `UCk_Utils_ProceduralRig_UE::Get_Clearance` returns the policy, `Get_ChainState` (`ECk_ProceduralRig_ChainState`: `Clear`, `Crossing`; without a policy and without sibling radii, always `Clear` because neither test runs) whether the last pose kept a link through a solid, into the slab or overlapping a sibling capsule, and `Get_SwivelDegrees` the stored angle.

**Sibling capsules.** The rig update gathers the live rigged legs of one ready body and settles their choices before publishing any part transform. A supplied radius defines a conservative capsule around each joint-to-joint segment. Empty radii preserve the legacy result; radii on `None` or disabled legs make their unchanged poses fixed obstacles. Only enabled `Swivel` legs whose legacy capsules overlap another participant generate sibling alternatives. Candidates retain the same hip and foot targets and cannot increase the baseline count of world-ray or body-slab crossings; this bounds crossing counts, not which particular links still cross. The pure `ck::SelectProceduralChainPoses` jointly reduces the sum of squared sibling penetration, with strict improvement and stable leg identities for ties. It accepts at most 64 legs, 24 poses per leg and 8 links per pose, and runs at most three selection passes per call. The adapter selects legacy poses, the coarse swivel fan, then a half-angle refinement only where overlap remains. Sibling candidate generation also tests each candidate against the existing world rays and slab; those extra link rays are included in the gait ray counter. These bounds are not a measured performance guarantee.

The gym authors each capsule radius from its actual rendered box cross section, `sqrt(HalfExtents.Y^2 + HalfExtents.Z^2)`, including the SmallCrawler4 visual scale of 0.6. Capsules conservatively enclose those boxes and include joint ends; their overlap is not a triangle-mesh intersection test. Sibling capsules do not sweep terrain or replace the existing zero-thickness Jolt link rays, query filter or approximate body slab. Bounded capsule candidate selection reduces overlap; residual proximal constraint conflicts and swing endpoint/path overlaps can remain `Crossing`, with world/slab and sibling crossing-link counts kept separately. The selector changes rig presentation only, keeping the simulation body and gait foot targets fixed. Its distal endpoint follows the actual foot in flight. Contact reservations protect new landings; they do not sweep or reroute the foot's flight path.

The gait's straight hip-to-foot occlusion line (see **Foothold search**) stays a veto on its targets: the rig clears the chain to a target the gait chose, it does not widen the targets the gait may choose.

## Disabling a leg

`UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::Request_EnableDisable` is reversible. A disabled leg leaves the step schedule and its foot freezes body-relative: the gait captures the foot pose in body space on the transition into `ck::FFragment_ProceduralLeg_FrozenPose`, present only while the leg is disabled, and re-expresses it every frame, so the rig keeps posing the leg and it rides rigidly with the body. Re-enabling removes the fragment. Disabled legs cost no Jolt queries; each solve clears their debug probe and trusted-contact readout while preserving the frozen foot pose. Survivors keep their indices; nothing is packed or re-indexed.

## Losing a leg

`UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::Request_Detach` is irreversible. In the leg's request drain it collects the parts the rig was posing, optionally transfers their lifetime ownership to the world (`ECk_ProceduralLeg_ReleasedPartsOwnership::TransferToWorld`), fires `OnProceduralLeg_Detached(Leg, ReleasedParts)` while the leg handle is still readable, then destroys the leg entity. The drain runs before the gait and rig updates, so released parts never receive another pose request. The gait masks the lost index and keeps running on the survivors.

Ownership stays with the body unless `TransferToWorld` is requested: destroying the body then destroys body-owned debris.

Ragdolling the released parts is the game's job. The recipe:

1. Bind `BindTo_OnDetached` on the leg, or read `UCk_Utils_ProceduralRig_UE::Get_Chain` first.
2. Call `Request_Detach`.
3. In the callback, for each released part: add a dynamic Jolt body with an explicit box shape sized like the part's visual (`FCk_JoltBody_Spec{ECk_JoltBody_ShapeSource::ExplicitShape}` with `FCk_Jolt_ShapeDimensions{ECk_Jolt_ShapeType::Box}` half-extents, `ECk_MotionType::Dynamic`, an explicit mass, then `UCk_Utils_JoltBody_UE::Add`). CkJolt does not read CkShapes. Give the body a collision profile the gait and surface-motion probes do not trace: the gym uses `Ragdoll` (PhysicsBody objects that ignore the Visibility channel the probes use). Released debris must never read as ground for the survivors.
4. Optionally push the parts with `UCk_Utils_JoltBody_UE::Request_AddImpulse` — only once `UCk_Utils_JoltBody_UE::Get_IsBodyAdded` is true, because an impulse before the body exists is dropped. The gym fixture (`CkProceduralAnimationGym_Fixture.as`) is the reference implementation of the Jolt body and the impulse. A CkTimer that destroys the debris later is an optional addition; the fixture does not add one.
5. Bind `UCk_Utils_ProceduralGait_UE::BindTo_OnLegSetChanged` to react to the new leg count: switch locomotion mode, or `Request_ApplyPreset` with a request built from a slower preset.

**Leg-loss policy.** `FCk_ProceduralGait_Timing::_LegLossPolicy` decides how survivors adapt. `KeepAuthoredOffsets` (default) keeps every leg's authored phase. `RedistributeOffsets` re-spaces the enabled legs evenly by index and blends there through the solver's pattern-blend machinery. With `_MaxSimultaneousSwings = 0` the swing budget is `max(1, enabled legs / 2)`, recomputed whenever the enabled set changes.

**Signals and live retune.** `OnProceduralGait_LegSetChanged(Gait, EnabledCount, TotalCount)` tracks the enabled set: it fires on the frame a leg is disabled, enabled, or detached or destroyed while enabled. Detaching or destroying an already-disabled leg leaves the enabled set unchanged and does not fire it. `TotalCount` is the number of legs the gait captured at `Add`, not the number still attached. `UCk_Utils_ProceduralGait_UE::Request_ApplyPreset` takes an `FCk_Request_ProceduralGait_ApplyPreset` (timing, step, probe and foothold, all four essential to its constructor, so a caller cannot leave the foothold search retuned to defaults by omission; a caller that built the request with three arguments before the foothold part existed must add the fourth; build it from a `UCk_ProceduralGait_Data` preset's `Get_Timing`, `Get_Step`, `Get_Probe` and `Get_Foothold`), copies those into the live gait and re-applies solver settings; leg state, planted feet, held footholds and the gait clock are kept.

## Footfall events

A leg fires `OnProceduralLeg_Planted(Leg, Footfall)` when its published foot phase changes to `Planted` and `OnProceduralLeg_Lifted(Leg, Footfall)` when it changes to `Swinging`; bind with `UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::BindTo_OnPlanted` / `BindTo_OnLifted` (default policy `FireIfPayloadInFlightThisFrame`, `DoNothing`) and unbind with the `UnbindFrom_*` twins. Only the gait update fires them, right after it writes the leg's foot, so a plant and a lift alternate per leg and each change fires once. Gait setup seeds every foot as planted without firing. `FCk_ProceduralLeg_Footfall` carries the foot's world position and normal, its `ECk_ProceduralLeg_FootContact` (on a plant, the plant's trust from the touchdown ray (see **Readiness**): a foot that touches down where that ray meets nothing, as a leg that gathers into the air past its contact grace does, plants `Guessed`; on a lift, whether the new target is ground its gait probed and trusts); the plant's normal is the touchdown ray's hit normal, or the support up when it met nothing, and `_LandingSpeed`: the foot's speed in cm/s over the frame it planted, 0 on a lift. There is no physical material: the Jolt hit carries none. Disabling a swinging leg freezes its foot as planted, and that transition fires a plant with a guessed contact; re-enabling fires nothing until the foot next lifts. A detached leg never fires: it is pending destruction from its request drain on, and the gait freezes its last pose without publishing a footfall.

## Body conform

`FCk_ProceduralBodyPose_Spec::_Conform` (`FCk_ProceduralBodyPose_Conform`: `_Mode`, `_MaxTilt` 20 degrees, `_HeightWeight` 0.5, `_MaxHeight` 10 cm, `_MaxTiltRate` 60 degrees per second, `_MaxHeightRate` 60 cm per second) is unpacked at `Add`: `PlantedFeet` adds the conform fragments, `None` adds neither, and `Add` rejects a max tilt outside 0..89 degrees, a height weight outside 0..1, a negative max height, or a max tilt rate or max height rate that is not finite and positive. Every frame the pure `ck::ComputeProceduralBodyConformPose` fits a weighted least-squares plane `h = a x + b y + c` through each captured foot's height above its rest height, at the foot's x and y in the simulation body's frame. Each foot counts by `ck_procedural_gait_utils::Get_SupportWeight`: a trusted plant fully, an untrusted plant (one that touched down where its gait found no ground it trusts) not at all, and a swinging foot fading out over the first third of its swing and back in over the last third, so the fit does not step when the planted set changes; a disabled, detached or destroyed leg counts zero, and so does every foot while the gait is airborne, because tucked feet stand on nothing (the fit is then underdetermined and the last one holds). The fit tilts the target's up onto the plane's normal, clamped to `_MaxTilt`, with no turn about the body's up, and moves it `_HeightWeight` times `c` along the body's up, clamped to `_MaxHeight`. The core returns `Fitted`, `Underdetermined` (fewer than three weighted feet, or weighted feet along one line: the update holds the last fitted target in the body's frame) or `Malformed` (a non-finite foot or a negative weight: the update ensures and latches `ECk_ProceduralBodyPose_Failure::MalformedConform`). A two-group walker with four legs holds during every swing and refits while all four stand. The target the springs chase is not the held fit itself but an applied target that the pure `ck::SlewProceduralBodyConformPose` moves toward it every frame, turning at most `_MaxTiltRate` and moving at most `_MaxHeightRate`: with feet on two faces of a sharp edge the plane swaps by up to 36 degrees in one frame, and the springs alone would still read that as a lurch. The critically damped rotation spring trails a target turning at a steady rate r by about 2 r / ω, with ω = sqrt(`_Spring._Stiffness` / `_Spring._Mass`), so keep 2 × `_MaxTiltRate` / ω within `_Spring._MaxAttitudeLag`: at the default stiffness 40 and mass 1, ω is 6.32 per second and a target slewing at the default 60 degrees per second is trailed by about 19 degrees, against the default 22. Both targets are body-local and live in the conform state fragment. The fit works in the body's frame, so it behaves the same on walls and ceilings, and the support-loss sag stays toward the surface the feet stand on; a game that wants gravity sag drives its own offset.

## Foot contact reservations and unsupported plants

Positive `FCk_ProceduralLeg_Spec::_FootContactRadius` reserves trusted planted points and trusted swing landing points against other enabled positive-radius legs of the same gait. The reserved landing is `FProceduralGaitLegSwing::Get_CommittedLandingPoint()`: the current landing XY and the higher Z of its admitted lift, when active. Disabled, detached or destroyed legs do not reserve space; a leg ignores its own reservation. Contacts exactly tangent at the sum of the radii are available. Reservations are rebuilt from current solver state rather than a second persistent contact store. Setup admits initial contacts in captured leg order and adds each admitted trusted plant before probing the next leg. Radius 0 disables contact reservations; untrusted-plant recovery remains independent of this opt-in radius.

Foothold hits still pass the existing reach, slope, own-side, UnderBody and occlusion gates; an overlapping hit reads `Reserved`. The existing search may find another contact. A reservation rejection is not terrain loss and cannot make the no-target gather silently plant at that occupied rest point. The pure solver also checks availability as swings start, retarget, freeze, lift and touch down, so legs processed later see already committed contacts. With a positive radius, trusted contact geometry is used directly instead of stroke overshoot, freeze displacement or damped target interpolation. A crowded existing plant may request a budgeted replacement, but it remains fixed until a real swing starts. There is no sliding or force-separating trusted anchors, and bounded search can leave a leg without a valid target.

Native touchdown admission applies to each actual Jolt hit after checking both simulation and ready posed-hip reach. The adapter rebuilds reservations before each touchdown, including earlier native corrections in that update. If the plant ray's hit is occupied or out of reach, the resolver can try the distinct validated-target ray; if neither hit passes, it keeps the original emitted point untrusted. Trust is not granted solely because the swing once had a trusted target. This contact reservation is a conservative sphere test, not a swept foot-mesh or terrain-volume collision test.

An enabled untrusted planted foot with a valid trusted replacement requests a real recovery swing even below the ordinary step threshold, including at rest. Recovery uses the existing simultaneous-swing budget and phase priority; it does not promote trust or move the plant in place. Its swing lands on the validated point without stroke overshoot or freeze push. Zero delta time does not advance recovery. Withdrawn landing ground cannot promote the plant, and the native touchdown admission remains decisive.

The gym clones each rig's leg specs per instance before `Add_Walker`; it never mutates the shared rig data asset. It authors the contact radius as `max(Get_FootHalfExtents(VisualScale).Size(), sqrt(TipHalfExtents.Y^2 + TipHalfExtents.Z^2))`: 14.352700 cm for the full-size `(10, 9, 5)` foot and 8.611620 cm for SmallCrawler4 at scale 0.6. `Create_WithRoster` has independent final options `InSiblingAvoidance` and `InFootReservations`; disabling sibling capsules does not disable contact reservations. Framework callers retain radius 0 unless they explicitly author it. Contact spacing can alter gait choices and throughput.

**Committed swing contact.** `DoBeginSwing` initializes `_LandingPoint` from the new target and clears the prior lift. Reservations and the feet-plane producer consume the same `Get_CommittedLandingPoint()` getter. The point includes a lift only after the solver admits it; an input probe report or predicted future landing cannot enlarge support merely because it found ground. This producer rule is independent of the authored SurfaceMotion height policy.

## Body height from the planted feet

With `FCk_SurfaceMotion_Contact::_HeightSource = PlantedFeet` the body rides on the plane through the ground its gait's feet stand on as well as on what its rays hit, so a walker whose feet stand on raised supports (pillar tops, posts, treads) stays on them instead of sinking into the gaps between them. With `Rays`, the default, nothing below applies and SurfaceMotion behaves exactly as without it.

- **The plane.** Every gait update, after the solve, gathers ground points from the solver's leg state, never a foot in flight: a planted leg contributes its plant; a swinging leg contributes the plant it left, weighing `1 - alpha`, and its landing point (`FProceduralGaitLegSwing::Get_CommittedLandingPoint()`, including only a landing lift the solver admitted), weighing `alpha`, `alpha` being the swing alpha. Each point supports the body by its trust (the plant's `_Trusted`; for the landing point, the trust the swing recorded for its target, `FProceduralGaitLegSwing::_TargetTrusted`, when the target was last accepted at take-off, on a retarget before the freeze or at the freeze) times `max(0, dot(normal, Up))` with the plant's normal or the swing's recorded `_TargetNormal` and the support up, so a foot gripping a face supports nothing (it holds the body sideways, not up) and one on a 45-degree slope 0.71; the point's weight in the fit is that support times its crossfade share. Only enabled, live legs count, and while the gait is airborne nothing does. The pure `ck::FitProceduralFeetPlane` (`Core/CkProceduralFeetPlane.h`, no world) fits the weighted least-squares plane `z = a x + b y + c` through the points in the support frame, relative to the body. The gait publishes it in world space, `Point = Body + Basis × (0, 0, c)` and `Normal = Basis × normalize(-a, -b, 1)`, with the frame (the body's rotation and location) and the footprint, the XY bounding box in that frame of every trusted point, whatever its alignment and its crossfade share (a swing on its take-off frame included), in `ck::FFragment_ProceduralGait_FeetPlane`. The footprint so reaches forward to where the feet are about to stand: a body walking onto tops its legs are aimed at stays inside it, and so does one whose legs ahead go to faces, though a face adds no height to the fit; at a true edge, where the legs ahead have no trusted target, it does not. The gait seeds its setup plants level (see **Core contract**), so until a leg first steps its plant weighs as level ground whatever it stands on.
- **States.** `Fitted` needs three or more weighted feet not along one line and a normal within 45 degrees of the support up (`ck::ProceduralFeetPlaneMaxAngleDegrees`): a steeper plane is a wall the feet happen to stand on, not ground the body can ride, and publishes `None`. Too few weighted feet (a two-group walker while one group swings) keep the last fitted plane, frame and footprint, in world space, as `Held` for at most 1.5 step durations since the fit, then `None`. A biped never has three feet down; compose it with `Rays`.
- **The rule.** SurfaceMotion reads the plane on its next update, one frame late (it runs in `FGroup_Physics`, the gait in `FGroup_Transform_Derived`), when its spec says `PlantedFeet` and the plane is not `None`, and passes it to `ck::StepProceduralSurfaceMotion`. In each substep the feet give a contact only while the candidate position, expressed in the plane's frame, lies inside the footprint grown by a quarter clearance on every side, and while the plane's normal lies within the same 45 degrees of SurfaceMotion's own support normal (the body may be turning onto a face its feet have not reached). The feet contact lies on the plane straight down the support normal, at the height `dot(Candidate - Point, N) / dot(Up, N)`, so a tilted plane is measured where the body is, not where the point is. The substep's down contact is the higher of the down ray's hit and the feet contact; a tie within 0.01 cm goes to the ray. The feet only ever lift the body: the ray floor keeps it out of the ground, where a body following its feet alone would sink into a hole it had not stepped into. The feet contact carries the down ray's normal when that ray hit, so a crease the feet span still turns the body onto the facet under it, and the feet plane's normal over a miss, where the ray tells nothing about the surface and the feet do; that normal goes through the same adoption and confirmation as any contact's. Over a pillar field's gap or a ledge's edge the plane agrees with the support and nothing turns; on a convex wall the feet stand on the wall and their plane turns the support back onto it as the body walks round, where the support it last saw would leave the rays missing the wall for good. The body pose's conform still tilts the drawn body toward the feet. While the feet contact exists the support holds, so a face ahead still waits for confirmation, and a down miss is not a miss: the look-ahead and the fan are cast only when neither contact exists, and the contact grace and the fall never start. Outside the footprint every rule of **Composition** item 2 applies unchanged. `Get_ContactSource` reports `Feet` while the feet contact holds the body.
- **Plant-time trust.** Which feet count is decided when they touch down. On the touchdown frame the gait casts one short ray through the plant along the normal of the ground the swing aimed at (10 cm in front of it to 10 cm behind, the ray a face hold is re-validated with, so a face plant is confirmed along its face and a top plant along its up): an admitted hit re-plants the foot at the hit, trusted, with the hit's normal, so a foot never stands off the surface that confirmed it (a landing clamped a few centimetres off a face, a plant a centimetre above a tread). When the first hit is missing or fails reach/contact admission, the gait casts the same ray once more through the target the swing validated (`FProceduralGaitLegSwing::_ValidatedTarget`), when that lies elsewhere: an admitted hit there re-plants the foot at that hit, trusted, with the hit's normal, since the last frame of a swing can carry a foot a centimetre or two past what was probed; no admitted hit leaves the foot where it landed, untrusted, with the support up, since a ray that met nothing tells nothing about the surface (`SetPlantedPose` records the plant; the decision is the pure `ck::ResolveProceduralTouchdown`, `Core/CkProceduralFootProbe.h`, no world). The trust holds for the whole stance: the solver writes it only at touchdown and the gait only through `SetPlantedPose`, never from the input while the foot is planted. At most two rays per touchdown, the second only when the first hit is missing or fails reach/contact admission. The published foot's `_Contact` reports the plant's trust while planted and this solve's foothold usability while swinging. A foot that stands on a pillar top keeps its trusted contact while its ideal target hangs over the gap beside it; a leg past its contact grace that gathers toward its rest target into the air plants untrusted and weighs 0, so feet dangling over a drop never hold the body up. Every consumer of the published foot reads this contract, the footfall signals and `Get_TrustedContactCount` included: a planted foot's `_Contact` is the ground it touched down on, not the ideal target's probe of the current solve.
- **Readouts.** `UCk_Utils_ProceduralGait_UE::Get_FeetPlane` returns `FCk_ProceduralGait_FeetPlane` (world point, normal and `ECk_ProceduralGait_FeetPlane`; `None` unless the gait is `Ready`), `UCk_Utils_SurfaceMotion_UE::Get_HeightSource` the spec's height source, and the debug snapshot both (see **Debugger**).

## Steps and walls

The forward ray is cast from the body along its travel, at the body's height, for one clearance, so it only ever meets a face whose top stands higher above the ground under the body than the body does. A face lower than the clearance (a stair's riser, a kerb, a 30 cm post under a 65 cm body) is never seen by it: the down ray lifts the body over it once it is under the body. `FCk_SurfaceMotion_Contact::_MaxStepHeight` and `_WallPolicy` decide what a face the forward ray does meet is; the defaults, 0 and `Climb`, climb every face as SurfaceMotion always did.

- **Step.** With a max step height above 0 (`Add` requires it above the clearance and at most the probe reach), a down ray is cast onto the face's top from the max step height plus one clearance above the face's hit and a quarter clearance past it, for the max step height plus two clearances. A trusted hit whose top lies above the ground under the body (the down ray's hit, or one clearance under the body when that ray found nothing) by more than 0 and at most the max step height, and whose normal lies within the confirm angle of the support normal, is a step: its top becomes the substep's down contact (`Get_ContactSource` reports `Step`), the support holds, and the face is not proposed. The body then rises toward one clearance over the top at the clearance speed with its support normal kept, and keeps moving while it rises: a face t high takes `(t - C) / ClearanceSpeed` to clear, over which the body travels its speed times that. At the gym's step height of 1.3 clearances, a 75 cm block met at 180 cm/s by a 65 cm body overlaps its front by about 5 cm, which the legs' clearance absorbs. A body longer than its clearance overruns a step as it overruns a wall it is confirming. Once the body has risen above the top, the forward ray passes over it and the look-ahead and the down ray carry the body on, so `Step` lasts only while the body is below the top. With planted feet the step's top is a down contact like the down ray's hit: the higher of it and the feet wins, and the feet then carry the step's normal. A body that already rides above the top (on its feet, or carried by the look-ahead from a raised top onto the next one) never sees the face and never steps. One more ray per face the forward ray meets.
- **Wall.** A face that is not a step is a wall. `Climb` confirms it and adopts it as the next support when the body has room on it (below; see **Composition** item 2). `Slide` never makes it support, and a wall without room is never support under either policy: such a face obstructs the body. Its standoff is the clearance, or, when a body one clearance off it would stand inside the solid across the gap (the room the free ray measured is shorter than the clearance), half that room, the gap's middle. Once the substep would leave the body closer to the face than its standoff, the travel loses its component into the face, so a glancing face slows the body and a head-on one stops it (the engine's `ComputeSlideVector`). The body is then pushed back out along the face's normal by at most the clearance speed's step, the rate every height correction uses, and the down ray and the rest are cast again from there (one more ray) as if no face had been seen. The obstruction moves only that substep's position: the support normal and the travel tangent stay as they were. The fan's face without room obstructs the body the same way while the travel points into it (an upright face the fan meets from beyond its edge faces along the travel, and the body walking away from it is not obstructed), and a fall landing without room loses the fall's velocity into its face and is kept off it too (below).
- **Following a wall.** The forward ray runs along the travel, so a face at an angle θ to it is first seen only once the body stands a clearance times sin θ off it (32.5 cm for a 65 cm body at 30 degrees). From there the body follows the wall. While the last substep was obstructed and the travel still points into the face, one ray is cast from the candidate along the face's normal, for the probe reach, before the forward ray's verdict:
  - a hit within the standoff keeps the obstruction and keeps the body off the face as above, so a body first seen close eases out to its standoff over a few substeps instead of jumping;
  - a hit beyond it keeps the obstruction and moves nothing;
  - a miss, or a steering that no longer points into the face, ends it.
  One ray per substep while obstructed, none otherwise. There is no steering: a body sliding into a head-on face stops there, and going around it is the caller's job, as it is for the engine's characters.
- **Room.** A contact is adopted only where the body has room on it: a free ray from the hit along its normal, from 1 cm to one clearance off the surface, meets nothing, or only the surface the body stands on within a quarter clearance (a concave corner, which stays a legitimate adoption). Every contact about to be adopted is checked once, one ray each: the forward ray's face (under either policy, since the room bounds the standoff), the step, the down contact, the look-ahead's, the fan's, the down contact a pending turn keeps under the body, and a fall's landing. The feet contact is no hit on a solid and is never checked. A contact without room is refused: nothing is proposed from it this substep, so the down contact under the body is adopted instead when it has room, and otherwise the look-ahead, the fan and the miss path run as after a down miss; a pending turn with no room under the body coasts; a face without room obstructs the body (above). A fall landing without room loses the fall's velocity into the refused face and falls on, down along it, kept off it at half the room, so a body falling into a gap narrower than its clearance lands on the gap's floor instead of standing inside the solid across it, and a body walking on across that floor stops in the gap's middle against the next face.
- **Readouts.** `UCk_Utils_SurfaceMotion_UE::Get_Obstruction` (`ECk_SurfaceMotion_Obstruction`) reads `Wall` while the last substep slid along a face, `None` otherwise, and `Get_ObstructionNormal` the face's normal (zero unless `Wall`); `Get_WallPolicy` and `Get_MaxStepHeight` read the Spec back. The debug snapshot carries all four (see **Debugger**).

## Reach and cadence

A leg's reach is the sum of its segment lengths. `FCk_ProceduralGait_Step` carries three fractions of it, validated as `0 < _TargetReachFraction < _ForceStepReachFraction <= 1` and `_ForceStepReachFraction <= _HardOverstretchReachFraction`, which lies in `[1, 1.5]` (defaults 0.8, 0.92 and 1.0); the solver holds them in `ck::FProceduralGaitReachSettings`. A step whose force-step and hard-overstretch fractions are both 1 is valid: every reach Emergency then also steps beyond the schedule.

- **Target clamp.** Every swing target stays within `_TargetReachFraction` of the hip: the take-off target (a catch step keeps its requested target, clamped), the damped retarget, the push at the retarget freeze phase, the target after the stroke overshoot (every frame, against the current hip), the swing a re-enabled leg begins, and the landing after airborne. A target on ground the caller validated (the leg input's `_TargetTrusted`) is the exception: the solver never moves it off that ground, so while it lies within `_ForceStepReachFraction` of the reach it is not clamped, the swing lands on it even beyond the target reach and the reach Emergency steps the leg again once the body has moved on; beyond the force-step reach it counts as no target that frame. The push and the overshoot are displacements of the target, and the landing they give is clamped as a whole, toward the hip, trusted target or not (`FProceduralGaitSolver::DoGet_DisplacedTarget`): the clamp keeps a foot's forward reach on a wall or a stair, and the landing-ground probe under the landing point verifies the result, so a landing clamped off a tread's edge finds no ground there and the swing drops the displacement or pulls back onto its validated target (see **Ground under the landing point**). A swing whose target's ground normal lies more than `ck::ProceduralGaitFaceAngleDegrees` (45) from the support up when the swing accepts the target (at take-off, on a retarget before the freeze, at the freeze) aims at a face, which has no tread to carry a foot along: the swing treats it as a foothold and lands on it where it lies, and keeps that classification for the rest of the swing (`FProceduralGaitLegSwing::_TargetOnAFace`), so a support frame that pitches while the foot is in the air cannot turn the face into ground and give it a displacement or a lift. `ck::FProceduralGaitSolver::ClampToReach` shrinks the planar offset in the support frame and keeps the height; a height alone beyond the limit clamps in 3D. Before probing, the gait clamps its query point (`Neutral + Lead`, with the velocity lead projected onto the support plane) the same way, so the probe looks for ground under a reachable point.
- **Ground within reach.** A probe hit farther than `_TargetReachFraction` of the reach from the hip is clamped the same way and probed once more there, so the target the solver receives is real ground within reach. The solver's clamp keeps a target's support-frame height, which across a convex bend (a crest, a ridge, a stair edge) lies inside the ground; the second probe keeps that clamp to trimming the stroke overshoot and the freeze push.
- **Unreachable ground holds the plant.** A final hit still farther than `_ForceStepReachFraction` of the reach from the hip is Unreachable, like a miss: the foothold search runs (see **Foothold search**); when it finds nothing, the plant holds through the contact grace, then gathers toward its rest target. The debug probe shows the final attempt. A foot never plants on ground far below a reachable point, as at a ledge or a gap. A hit whose normal faces along the ray (a back face) is never trusted either.
- **Reach Emergency.** A planted foot farther than `_ForceStepReachFraction` of the reach from its hip is an Emergency, joining the error Emergency (`Error > Threshold × EmergencyFactor`) with its rules: it needs a valid target, ignores the swing window and the budget, and waits for inhibition (unless it is also hard-overstretched, below). A planted foot whose hip cannot see it (the leg input's `_PlantOccluded`) is an Emergency of ratio 1, so an over-reach or a large error still outranks it in the priority choice; with a valid target it steps beyond the schedule like a hard-overstretched foot (below).
- **Priority.** The planted Emergency leg whose phase group has no swing in flight, with the largest ratio `max(Error / (Threshold × EmergencyFactor), |Plant − Hip| / (ForceStepFraction × Reach))`, holds back every take-off in the other groups, their Emergency take-offs included, until it steps. Without it an Emergency leg starves: while a group swings, inhibition keeps it planted, and on the frame that group drains, leg index order hands the slot to another group. While the body turns in place every group is in Emergency at once, so exempting Emergency take-offs would starve one group for seconds. Catch steps of other groups are not held back and inhibition is unchanged. A valid-target Emergency may join an already active ordinary swing in its own phase group despite another group holding priority, only while uninhibited and within the swing budget, and only when that peer has at least half an ordinary swing duration remaining. The peer's deadline is captured after its projected advance for this update; the join starts at phase zero with a duration scale that finishes at that existing deadline rather than extending the group. Catch-step and beyond-schedule peers supply no join deadline. Other take-offs retain their original duration and reservation rules.
- **Hard overstretch.** A planted foot farther than `_HardOverstretchReachFraction` of the reach from its hip, with a valid target, steps beyond the phase schedule: it takes off although another group swings or holds priority. A body that climbs away from its planted feet (up a pillar, onto a beam end or a ledge) stretches every group's floor feet at once, and the schedule would step them one group after another; a 16-leg centipede's last group waited a whole cycle, 0.3 to 0.4 s, up to 1.6 chain lengths out. The swings it starts form a second lane beside the schedule, marked `FProceduralGaitLegSwing::_BeyondSchedule`: they neither inhibit the schedule's take-offs nor wait for them, and only one phase group swings in the lane at a time, so at most two groups ever swing, one on the schedule and one beyond it. A take-off beyond the schedule needs the swing budget, unlike an Emergency on it; a lane swing still counts toward the budget and still keeps its group out of the priority choice. A pending catch step waits for the schedule as before. An occluded plant with a valid target takes off in the same lane under the same budget and one-group rule: every frame it waits, the body carries it deeper behind the solid, and at travel speed the other group's reach Emergencies would hold its ratio of 1 back for longer than a step. The rule cannot step a foot whose target the contact grace withholds: the floor below a raised hip usually lies beyond the force-step reach, so a floor foot holds through the grace first. At the default of 1 the rule never fires on a straight walk, where planted feet stay within 0.94 of their chains at up to 260 cm/s.
- **Turning.** The gait reads the body's turn rate from the twist, about the body's up, of its frame-to-frame rotation (`ck::FProceduralGaitYawRateTracker`), so a tilt step at a facet or a wall corner reads as no turn; the rate is signed and low-passed over 0.1 s. Its magnitude times the mean rest-foot radius adds to the cadence speed. Its sign drives the yaw term of Raibert placement: before the reach clamp, the query point `Neutral + Lead` is turned about the body location by the rate times `_StepDuration`, the same time the linear lead uses, so a turning body's feet land ahead of the turn; the whole lead, travel and turn together, then moves the query point at most `_MaxVelocityLead` from its neutral (`ck::FProceduralGaitSolver::ComputeLeadQuery`). The cap keeps a fast turner's lead in proportion (240 degrees per second times a 0.35 s step is 84 degrees) and bounds what a one-frame heading jump, such as SurfaceMotion's re-seat after a fall, can do to the targets while the low-passed rate carries it. The push at the retarget freeze still uses the planar velocity alone and ignores the turn.
- **Ground under the landing point.** On every swing frame the core exposes the point the swing will land on, after the stroke overshoot and the reach clamp (`FProceduralGaitLegSwing::_LandingPoint`): on the take-off frame, the swing target; before the retarget freeze, the point the freeze will produce from this frame's ideal target advanced by one frame of planar travel, because the damped target trails a moving ideal, the freeze snaps it forward (by about 13 cm at 180 cm/s) and the probe's report reaches the solver a frame later; from the freeze on, the actual landing point. The freeze and this prediction share one solver function. The gait probes under that point like the clearance probe, along the support down from `_Probe._Up` above, trusting no back face and no hit farther than `_ForceStepReachFraction` of the reach from the hip, and tells the next frame's solve what it found as the leg input's `_LandingGround` (`ck::EProceduralGaitLandingGround`): `Found` with the hit's height in `_LandingGroundZ`, `None` when the probe found no such ground, and `Unknown` when it cast none (a planted leg, a catch step, and a swing whose target is on a face, `_TargetOnAFace`, which has no tread to lift onto). `_LandingGroundZ` means something only with `Found`. When that ground lies above the landing target and the lifted point stays within `_TargetReachFraction` of the reach, the swing lifts onto it: the rest of the arc rises by the difference in step with the swing's own easing, from the alpha the lift began at, so the foot reaches the upper tread at touchdown without a jump. A lifted swing follows the latest `Found` report, down to no lift when a later one finds no ground above, so a landing point that moves back off the upper tread never plants hovering; any other report keeps the last lift. **No ground under the landing point** (`None`) means the stroke overshoot and the freeze push carry the foot past the ground the gait validated, off a top's edge into the air: told so before its retarget freeze, the swing gives up both for the rest of the swing, exactly as a swing aimed at a foothold does, and lands on its validated target; told so first at or after the freeze, the swing's landing point is pulled back from where it stood to the target the freeze took before its push (`FProceduralGaitLegSwing::_ValidatedTarget`, captured once: the take-off target, then the freeze's, never re-derived from a later input), over the rest of the swing in step with its own easing, and the arc's height is unchanged. A swing keeps the target it was given: the gait keeps a swinging leg's hold while the hold stays usable, and an input with no valid target leaves the swing's target, its recorded trust and its normal as they are, so nothing after take-off moves a foot already on its way except a retarget before the freeze. The touchdown's trust and normal come from the gait's touchdown ray (see **Body height from the planted feet**), not from this report, which describes the landing point of the solve before. A catch step never lifts. The overshoot and the freeze push can carry a target probed on a lower tread past the next riser, which is what this lifts out of the upper tread. A report that first arrives on the touchdown frame lifts the plant only when the rise is at most `FProceduralGaitReachSettings::_TouchdownLiftFraction` of the step height (0.08 by default, 2 cm at a 25 cm step; `ValidateSettings` keeps it in `[0, 1]`); a larger one is left below that ground and counted (`FProceduralGaitSolver::Get_MissedLandingLifts`, also in the debug snapshot). The gait's touchdown ray gives the plant the normal of the ground it stands on. The debug snapshot records each leg's landing-ground probe and the point it was cast under.
- **Reach-safe touchdown.** The final ground-confirmation ray admits a trusted planted contact only when its actual hit lies within the full physical chain reach of both the simulation hip and a ready body-pose presentation hip. If the hit through the swing plant is out of reach, the gait still tests the previously validated target; if neither hit is reachable, it keeps the original landing untrusted rather than publishing an impossible support anchor. The debug snapshot foot pose is copied from the published leg foot after this resolution, while swing target and phase offset remain solver diagnostics.
- **Cadence floor.** For each enabled leg, with `L` its reach, `dz` the hip's height over the rest foot and `lateral` the rest foot's body-Y offset from the hip: `R_t = sqrt(max(0, (TargetFraction × L)² − dz²))` and `R_f` likewise with `ForceStepFraction`. The leg strides along body X over the chord `U = sqrt(R_t² − lateral²) + sqrt(R_f² − lateral²)`, and is skipped when `lateral >= R_t`. With `Ts = CycleDuration − StepDuration`, the solver runs with `min(authored CadenceSpeedRef, 0.85 × min U / Ts)`, which keeps stance travel per cycle within 85 % of the shortest chord. The floor is rebuilt at `Add`, on `Request_ApplyPreset` and whenever the enabled set changes. An authored reference of 0 (cadence scaling off) stays 0; `Ts <= 0` or no striding leg leaves the authored value. Admission keeps every rest foot inside the target radius, so a skipped leg can only sit exactly on it. The chord ignores the velocity lead: a swing lands at most `_MaxVelocityLead` ahead of the rest foot, so a lead shorter than the chord's forward part leaves a shorter usable stride and a floor more permissive than the chord suggests. For the gym's walkers the floor is above the authored reference, so it changes nothing there; it guards layouts whose legs cannot cover the stance travel.

## Foothold search

Every enabled leg chooses its foot target this way on every update, and once at setup so a search pick is held from the first update on. Down rays are the ideal probe's ray under a point (`_Probe._Up` above to `_Probe._Down` below along the trace axis, leaned outward on the first attempt); every hit is validated the same way and becomes a candidate with a verdict (`ck::EProceduralFootholdVerdict`):

- `Miss`: no trusted hit (a back face and an inside-solid origin are never trusted).
- `Unreachable`: farther than `_ForceStepReachFraction` of the reach from the hip. A down-ray hit beyond `_TargetReachFraction` is first clamped toward the hip and probed once more there, as the ideal target always was, except under a held foothold, which is judged at its own point (step 1); a face hit beyond it (the occluder's face, an inward or outward cast) is `Unreachable` at once, because a down ray there would find a different surface. That second probe casts straight down, without the first attempt's outward lean, so it lands under the clamped point: leaning like the first, it could clip the same edge again. When it finds nothing (the clamped point hangs over a gap past a top's edge), or only ground out of reach, the candidate stays `Unreachable` and the search runs.
- `TooSteep`: for search candidates only, a normal more than `_Foothold._MaxAngle` from the support up. The ideal target and a held foothold accept any surface they do not see from behind, as the probe always did.
- `Inboard`: for search candidates only, a hit farther than a tenth of the reach inboard of the hip along the leg's own side (the direction from the hip to its rest foot in the support plane; `ck::Get_IsFootholdOnOwnSide`). A foothold there folds the leg under the body: a beam's side under the belly, a log's lower facet. The ideal target and a held foothold are not tested (the ideal is the rest foot plus the lead; a hold was a search candidate when it was chosen). A leg whose rest foot lies straight under its hip has no own side and is never `Inboard`. Gripping a surface under the body needs a pole that folds the leg inward, a rig question outside this module's scope; such a leg gathers at rest instead.
- `UnderBody`: for search candidates only, a hit closer to the body's centreline than its widest enabled hip less 5 cm (in the support frame, measured from the body) and deeper below its leg's hip than a quarter of the leg's rest drop (`ck::Get_IsFootholdUnderBody`). The own-side rule cannot see this for a leg whose rest direction runs along the body: a front or back leg of a crawler over a beam would otherwise hold the beam's side under the body's middle, and a beast would hold a log's lower facets under its belly. Such a leg folds under the body and its stretched hold re-steps beyond the schedule. The hips are taken to sit on the body's surface; hips inset from it make the rule slightly permissive, the safe side. The ideal target and a held foothold are not tested. A beam narrower than the hips is a course the search has no answer for: feet on its top would need a preference for the surface under the body, feet on its sides a rig whose pole folds the leg inward.
- `Occluded`: a line trace from the hip, with the probe's query filter, meets a solid farther than `_Foothold._OcclusionTolerance` from the hit. A trace that starts inside a solid (fraction 0, a hip inside the body's own collider) tells nothing and occludes nothing. The straight line is a proxy for the chain: a foot beyond an edge the chain would drape over reads occluded, and the search then offers the edge's face and the floor in front of it.
- `Usable` otherwise.

No verdict tests the swing itself: a foot may swing through a solid between its plant and its target, such as a pillar corner between a foot and the floor beyond it. Going around needs swing-path planning the gait does not have; a veto on targets whose swing meets a solid, with the plant held while nothing is usable, left feet stuck behind the solids the body walked past.

1. **Held foothold.** A leg that holds a foothold (`ck::FProceduralFootholdState` in `_Footholds`, in world space, never rotated with the support frame) re-validates it every solve, with one ray and the occlusion trace. The ray follows how the hold was found (`ck::EProceduralFootholdCast` on the state): a hold a down ray found (`Ring`, the `Front` floor, the face-guided `Inward` top) is checked with one down ray under it (the first attempt, the second only when the first starts inside a solid), and the reach and occlusion checks apply to that hit as it is: never clamped toward the hip and probed again like the ideal target, which would stand a different point in for the hold and, on continuous ground, never let a trailing hold read `Unreachable`; a hold a face cast found (the `Front` face, the face-cast `Inward`, `Outward`) is checked with one ray along its own normal, from 10 cm in front of it to 10 cm behind, because a down ray under a point on a vertical face runs along the face and never meets it. A hold that is no longer usable is cleared.
2. **Ideal target.** The probe under the ideal target, three attempts as before, then the occlusion trace. When the first, leaned attempt of any down probe (the ideal, a down-cast hold, a ring point, the floor in front of an obstacle) meets a face (more than `ck::ProceduralGaitFaceAngleDegrees` from the support up), one straight ray is cast under the same point, and level ground it meets within `_ForceStepReachFraction` of the reach is the candidate instead: the lean reaches out past the point and can meet the face of a pillar or a riser beside the floor or the tread under it. Over a gap the straight ray finds nothing in reach and the face stands. One more ray per face hit. A swinging leg keeps a usable hold whatever the ideal says: the choice between hold and ideal is made at take-off, and in flight only once the target has become unusable. Otherwise a planted leg normally keeps a usable hold within the keep radius of the ideal target (`Held`), whether the ideal is usable or not, so the foot does not hop between two spots that agree. This early retention is bypassed when the ideal is occluded by an actual hit, or is a usable face admitted by the existing max-angle gate whose normal differs from the held surface by the existing face-angle test. These cases enter the existing budgeted search; a usable hold remains fallback if the budget or back-off prevents searching, or no better target is found. Outside those exceptions, beyond the keep radius a usable ideal is the target (`Ideal`) and clears the hold. A usable ideal on a face (its normal more than `ck::ProceduralGaitFaceAngleDegrees` from the support up, the edge of a top the leaned attempt met from the side) does not pre-empt the tops around it: up to the search it is treated as unusable, and the search runs (step 3). With the ideal unusable but not occluded, a usable hold within `_ForceStepReachFraction` of the reach of it (the leg's own usable radius) serves as well while it lies ahead of the hip along the body's travel in the support plane, or while the body is at rest: an ideal hanging beside a beam or over a gap would otherwise drop, on the next solve, the hold the leg's own search found, and the leg would search, back off and gather into the air in turn; and a pick beside a moving ideal would be searched again every frame and the target would creep forward with the body. A hold beyond the keep radius and behind the hip along the travel, one the leg has walked on past (a top 50 cm behind the hip while the next top lies in reach), does not stop the search: it competes in it as one more candidate at its own distance from the ideal (step 3), so a spot beside the ideal beats it and a hold near the ideal keeps winning. A hold farther than `_ForceStepReachFraction` of the reach from the ideal is cleared instead: the body has walked away from it (up a log, over a crest) and a leg kept on it re-plants behind its rest point. The ideal target is never held, so where it always works no hold exists.
3. **Search**, when the ideal target is unusable or usable on a face and no usable hold serves, including the planted hold exceptions in step 2. Up to 16 new search candidates, in this order: when the ideal target was `Occluded`, two `Front` ones, the face the occlusion trace met and a down ray a tenth of the reach from it back toward the hip (the floor in front of the obstacle); two `Inward` casts from the ideal target toward `Hip − Up × 1.5 RestDrop` and `Hip − Up × 4 RestDrop`, extended by `_Probe._Down` (a pillar side facing the leg, the floor under a raised hip, a cylinder side curving away); if an inward hit reads `UnderBody`, one additional inward cast at that depth preserves the ideal point's lateral spacing instead of converging it onto the hip, for at most two spread retries per searched leg, still through the unchanged admission gates; one `Outward` cast from `Hip + Up × 2 RestDrop` through the ideal target, extended by `_Probe._Down` (a pillar top or face beyond it); when the ideal is on a face, one straight `Inward` down ray a short distance inside that face (the greater of 1 cm and 0.15 of the search radius) to sample a shallow top before the ring; eight `Ring` down rays on a circle of the search radius around the ideal target in the support plane (first attempt, second only from inside a solid). The ideal-face top probe keeps its query and position in this order. When it did not run and the completed selection chooses a usable face normal, one straight top probe is appended inside that chosen face and the same selector runs again. This shares one top-probe opportunity per searched leg; a top that ties the old winner stays behind it. Only this appended probe caps its preferred inset to half the positive lateral distance to the existing UnderBody boundary, in the support frame, when moving inside the face approaches that boundary; a step no larger than 0.001 cm is skipped. Every resulting hit still passes the ordinary reach, slope, own-side, UnderBody and occlusion validation. Refining an Ideal or Held face reports a newly searched Inward top; other sources retain their provenance. Its down cast determines how a resulting hold is revalidated. `RestDrop` is the leg's hip height over its rest foot. The casts centre on the leg's own hip, not the body's centre, so the end legs of a long body look under themselves rather than along it. The pure `ck::SelectProceduralFoothold` (`Core/CkProceduralFoothold.h`, no world) picks the least-cost `Usable` one of the search's candidates in the support frame, `cost = |p − ideal| / reach + SlopeWeight × (1 − n·Up) + (planted ? ContinuityWeight × |p.z − plant.z| / reach : 0)`, ties to the earlier candidate. For an occluded ideal, distance costs use the actual ideal hit. Only when its occluder is a face admitted by the existing max-angle gate does ranking use the normalized midpoint of the support up and that obstruction normal as its slope reference; this ranks the obstruction and current support evenly without changing probe directions, the support frame, or reach, slope, own-side, UnderBody and occlusion validation. A usable face ideal retains the ordinary slope reference so nearby tops can still beat it. A usable hold beyond the keep radius and behind the hip competes with them, placed first, and so does a usable face ideal, placed next, with the distances measured from the face ideal, so its cost is its slope term and continuity term alone (0.5 for a vertical face with the default weight): a top within half the reach of it wins, and the face stays the target where no top is in reach (the Pillars, the Posts, a cylinder). A hold the force-step bound cleared is not among them, so it is never picked again. A search pick is the target and becomes the leg's held foothold; when the hold wins it stays the `Held` target, and when the face ideal wins it stays the `Ideal` target and the hold is cleared, and either way the leg backs off the search as after one that found nothing. A swing aimed at a searched or held foothold (the leg input's `_TargetIsFoothold`: any source but `Ideal` and `None`) gets neither the stroke overshoot nor the freeze push, so it lands on the spot the search validated. **Budget:** per update at most `max(4, enabled legs / 2)` legs run this step; the update visits the legs from the one after the last leg that searched (`_NextSearchLeg` on the gait fragment), so the searches go round. A leg past the budget, or one whose last search found nothing better within the last three solves (`_SearchedSolve` on its foothold state), keeps a hold behind the hip as the target, else its face ideal, else takes step 4 this solve. The held re-validation and the ideal probe are never budgeted, and setup searches every leg. Because a hold within the keep radius or ahead of the hip serves without a search, and a search that finds nothing better backs off, the budget binds only while many legs fail together.
4. **Nothing usable.** The foot is untrusted and the contact grace runs as before, with one change: past its grace a leg gathers toward its rest target only when the ideal target was missed or out of reach. When the ideal target was occluded the plant holds, with no valid target, until a solve finds a foothold: a foot never swings toward a point its leg cannot reach without passing through a solid.
5. **Occluded plant.** Every planted, enabled leg traces from its hip to its plant each update, with the same filter and tolerance. An occluded plant is an Emergency that steps beyond the schedule (see **Reach and cadence**), so a foot the body carries behind a post steps free within about one step.
6. **Landing lift.** A swing onto a face is not lifted (see **Ground under the landing point**).

**Tunables.** `FCk_ProceduralGait_Foothold` (on the preset, in the tunables fragment and on the apply-preset request): `_SearchRadius` (cm; 0 derives 0.3 of the leg's reach), `_KeepRadius` (cm; 0 derives 1.5 step thresholds; how close a usable hold must lie to the ideal target to stay the target without a search, whether the ideal is usable or not; beyond it, a hold behind the hip along the travel and within the force-step reach of the ideal competes in the search), `_MaxAngle` (90 degrees, in [0, 90], search candidates only), `_SlopeWeight` (0.5), `_ContinuityWeight` (0.25) and `_OcclusionTolerance` (3 cm); every field finite and non-negative. A radius of 0 meaning "derive" follows the timing's `_CadenceSpeedRef` and `_MaxSimultaneousSwings`.

**Ray budget.** A rig with a clearance policy adds its link rays (see **Chain clearance**). In steady state a leg casts its ideal probe (one ray unless an attempt starts inside a solid, one more when its leaned attempt meets a face), the ideal target's occlusion trace and, while planted, the plant trace; a swinging leg adds its clearance and landing rays, and a touchdown one ray, two when the first yields no admitted hit. So every enabled leg casts two traces more than before the search existed. A search costs up to 16 new candidates, including at most two UnderBody-triggered inward spread retries, the down rays up to three attempts each plus a straight retry and a clamped re-probe, and one occlusion trace each. The shared face-top opportunity costs at most five counted rays: two inside-solid attempts, two clamped re-probe attempts and the occlusion trace; a selected-face refinement adds this work only when the ideal-face probe did not run. A held leg also probes its ideal target every solve. The budget bounds a solve's searches to `max(4, enabled legs / 2)` legs, eight on a 16-leg walker, and the back-off keeps a leg whose search found nothing better from repeating it on the next three solves; a hold on a face costs one ray, like one on a floor.

**Readouts.** The published foot's `_Foothold` (`ECk_ProceduralLeg_Foothold`, through `UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::Get_Foot`) names the source of this solve's target, and `UCk_Utils_ProceduralLeg_UE::Get_IdealVerdict` the verdict of its ideal target. `UCk_Utils_ProceduralAnimation_Debug_UE::Get_RaysLastSolve(Gait)` (Blueprint and AngelScript; 0 for an invalid handle) returns the rays the gait's last setup or update cast, plus the link rays its rigs with a clearance policy cast that frame. The debug snapshot's leg keeps every candidate the solve validated (`_Footholds`: world position, normal, source and verdict), the chosen index, the source and whether the plant was occluded; its gait keeps the ray count.

## Scheduling and ownership

SurfaceMotion processes setup, steering and movement in `FGroup_Physics`, in that order: surface-motion setup, steering requests, update. A steering request enqueued on the `Add` frame lands after the setup seed, so the request wins. Its deferred root transform is settled by the ordinary transform pass. In `FGroup_Transform_Derived` the order is: leg requests (enable/disable, detach), gait setup and gait requests (apply preset), gait update, then body pose update and rig setup (both after the gait update), then rig update. The rig reads the offset the body pose computed this frame; the presentation entity's transform request is applied afterwards by the derived-transform settle barrier. Gait requests do not wait for setup: a preset applied before setup lands. The existing derived-transform settle barrier applies limb outputs before post-transform render consumers. Pending requests on a destroyed leg or gait complete `Failed_Cancelled` in `FGroup_EndPlay`.

No processor is parallel. Gait, SurfaceMotion and a rig with a clearance policy issue Jolt ray casts, and the Jolt query API is game-thread-only, so the rig update stays serial.

SurfaceMotion must be the sole movement owner when composed. Do not also integrate root velocity through another mover. Gait can instead consume a root driven by another system without SurfaceMotion. Its ground provider is CkJolt; purely Chaos collision that is absent from Jolt will not produce foot contacts.

All `Request_*` functions carry the Ck completion delegate. Invalid admission ensures, fires `Failed_NotEnqueued` and attaches no partial feature state. Request completion reports local processing, not network delivery.

## Core contract

`ck::FProceduralGaitSolver` and its swing/probe helpers contain no world or UObject operations. Coordinates use a support-aligned frame with +Z as up. This is a rotation about the world origin, not a moving body-local origin. Re-express stored state with `NewBasis.Inverse() * OldBasis`; sample translation velocity in world space before rotating it. Otherwise turning the support frame manufactures false velocity or moves planted feet.

All durations use FCk_Time; phase remains dimensionless. Explicit Reset establishes topology. Step rejects mismatched arrays, malformed settings and nonfinite inputs without partial mutation. Non-advancing evaluation uses a solver copy, preserving internal state. Core rejection results must be handled by adapters. Each leg input carries an enabled flag; disabled legs keep their index and are skipped by scheduling, take-off, landing and the airborne tuck. Each input also carries `_Hip` (support frame) and `_Reach` (centimetres); a reach of 0 disables the target clamp and the reach Emergency for that leg, so pure-core callers that set neither keep the reach-free behaviour. The priority rule applies regardless of reach. `_LandingGround` (`Unknown`, `None`, `Found`) reports the ground under the swing's landing point and `_LandingGroundZ` its support-frame height, read only with `Found`; with a reach of 0 the swing lifts onto it without a reach check. `None` drops the stroke overshoot and the freeze push before the freeze and pulls the landing back onto `_ValidatedTarget` after it. `_PlantOccluded` marks a planted foot whose hip cannot see it; with a valid target it is an Emergency of ratio 1 that steps beyond the schedule. `_TargetIsFoothold` marks a target the caller validated as a spot to stand on; a swing whose target is on a face when the swing accepts it (its `_GroundNormal` more than `ProceduralGaitFaceAngleDegrees` from +Z) is treated the same way for the rest of the swing (`FProceduralGaitLegSwing::_TargetOnAFace`); a swing aimed at either gets neither the stroke overshoot nor the freeze push, and once it is aimed at one before its freeze the overshoot stays off for the rest of that swing. `_TargetTrusted` (default true) marks a target on ground the caller probed and trusts: while within `_ForceStepReachFraction` of the reach it is never reach-clamped, beyond it the input counts as having no valid target. A pure-core caller that never sets it therefore no longer sees its targets clamped to the target reach within the force-step reach, a change against the earlier release; a caller that hands over unprobed geometry sets it false. A catch step's target is always untrusted, whatever the input says. The swing records its target's trust and normal when it accepts the target (`FProceduralGaitLegSwing::_TargetTrusted`, `_TargetNormal`); an input with no valid target leaves the swing's target, trust and normal unchanged. At touchdown the plant's `FProceduralGaitLegPlant::_Trusted` is true with a `Found` landing report, false with `None`, and with `Unknown` the swing's recorded trust; the plant takes the recorded normal. It holds until the next touchdown; the gait then confirms every touchdown with its own ray and `SetPlantedPose`. `Reset` takes an optional trust flag per foot (empty trusts every foot, another count is rejected) and `SetPlantedPose` one for its foot; a disabled leg's frozen pose is never trusted. `Reset` records every plant with the support up as its normal; the gait's setup publishes the probed normal on its feet, and the solver's plants take their ground's normal from their first touchdown on.

`ck::ComputeProceduralSwingPoint` (`Core/CkProceduralGaitSwingProfile.h`) returns the point the solver's swing from a start to a target passes at a swing phase, lifted along a given up by a given height: the swing settings' authored ease and arc where valid, else the solver's smooth-step ease and its parametric arc at the settings' apex phase and sharpness. The solver's sprint height scale, obstacle clearance and landing lift are not in it. The gait does not call it yet.

Foot probe states distinguish current hits from fallback targets. A brief miss withholds a target to hold the plant; after the duration-based grace expires, the foot may gather toward its fresh rest target. Guessed targets are never counted as trusted surface contacts. Retry spans are bounded and remove outward lean on fallback attempts, supporting both convex and concave geometry.

The ECS facade interprets MaxSimultaneousSwings = 0 as max(1, enabled leg count / 2); the pure core interprets zero as unlimited. Emergency and catch steps may exceed the nominal swing budget.

## Scope

This release targets rigid N-leg rigs with 1..8 segments per leg and static surfaces. It does not claim arbitrary surface pathfinding, moving-platform attachment, skeletal/ControlRig integration, combat sequencing, adding legs after the gait exists, or replicated/persisted simulation state. Gait pattern tables, settle and airborne tuning exist in the core but are not exposed on the data asset yet. The CkTests courses use authored patrol steering; their motion and articulation still run through production features.

## Provenance

Adapted from the user's AlchemyAnimus checkout in Venus at commit `7114d161277e60639ad6a15262877e34bb5c3368` (2026-09-24 audit). The core preserves scheduling, inhibition, emergency/catch stepping, cadence, settling, landing, swing profiles and support-frame transport. Changes include domain-typed time, explicit rejection contracts, nonmutating preview, duration-based contact loss and index-stable leg disabling (Animus packs survivors instead). The surface-motion ECS implementation is a revised design, not a copy of the source Actor driver.

Reference source: `AlchemyGaitSolver`, `AlchemyGaitSwingProfile`, `AlchemyGaitFootProbe`, `AlchemyAnimusGaitDriverComponent`; reference showcase: `AlchemyAnimusCompositeGym` and `AlchemyAnimusPartTestWalker`. Existing source AnimGraph walking uses another solver and is not part of this port.

## Verification

Tests live in CkTests under `Private/UnitTests/CkProceduralAnimation/` and `Script/CkProceduralAnimation`. The registered gym is **Procedural Animation Surface Traversal** in the shared gym switchboard. It shows nine 4/6/8-leg rigs across uneven ground, ramp/wall and concave-loop courses. P controls travel, R resets, V toggles contact diagnostics, K disables or re-enables leg 0 of every crawler, J detaches leg 1 of the first crawler and ragdolls its parts (one-shot), Home frames the overview, 1/2/3 frame the courses. Each crawler's body is drawn on a body-pose presentation entity, so the crawler body sags toward disabled/detached legs (K/J). The 4-leg crawlers use a `RedistributeOffsets` preset, the 6- and 8-leg crawlers the default `KeepAuthoredOffsets`; a crawler that drops below three enabled legs applies the slow preset through `Request_ApplyPreset`. **N** cycles the same three course slots through five walker sets and resets them: the original crawlers above; a menagerie of a spider (8 legs x 4 segments), a tentacled walker (6 x 8, the rig's maximum) and a mixed-chain beast (2-segment front legs, 4-segment rear legs, trotting) on stairs, rubble and a convex hump; and three stress sets with course-specific rosters, a step field with 10 to 40 cm risers, a 150 cm ledge and a sharp 90-degree ridge, then a log of radius 80 cm, a 30 cm beam per walker and a ramp onto a field of 50 cm pillar tops 150 cm high and down again, then posts in every lane, 75 and 30 cm tall in turn with 130 cm of floor between them, between 90 cm flanks, three isolated native Jolt cylinders 5 m tall and radius 90 cm, spaced 800 cm apart, authored for a spider, a biped and a separate four-leg crawler at 60 percent of the full-size crawler's hip placement, chain lengths, clearance, body and foot geometry. Their routes circle upward and downward between 160 and 400 cm without leaving the course (and steer back to the wall after contact loss). The pillar field has eleven lane-local 120 cm square blocks per walker, spaced 160 cm apart with alternating 20 cm lateral offsets and tops at 60, 100, 140, 180, 200, 200, 200, 180, 140, 100 and 60 cm. Every lane centreline crosses these blocks instead of running through a corridor between rows. The rigid, unsegmented centipede is omitted from interactive gym and PaViz rosters; its species implementation and dedicated framework regression tests remain available. The PaViz harness also spins walkers in place; the gym does not. Every walker in the menagerie and stress sets is composed through `Add_Walker` from rig and gait data assets plus a body pose that conforms to its planted feet; SurfaceMotion turns every live gym walker at 240 degrees per second, and uses the existing species height policy: `PlantedFeet` except the biped, whose two feet never fit a plane and use `Rays`. Gym fixtures default to `Species`; explicit `Rays` and `PlantedFeet` overrides remain authoritative. Cylinder uses the same policy as the other courses, and its drawn body still conforms to planted feet; every walker steps onto faces up to 1.3 of its clearance, and the beast slides along the faces it cannot step onto (`Slide`) while every other species climbs them, so the beast is on neither the ledge nor the log; the 6-leg crawler sets its chains to `Fabrik` and the tentacled walker to `Curve`, the rest use `Auto`; every live gym species' rigs use the `Swivel` clearance. K, J and 1/2/3 act on whichever set is live. Source presence and UHT generation do not prove runtime traversal or visual quality.

`CirclesCylinderUpAndDown` retains two observed up/down cycles, at least 80 percent trusted body-wall coverage and per-leg trusted wall plants; it separately rejects neighboring-cylinder plants and guessed planted feet held more than 5 cm from the own closed cylinder and floor in the middle helix band. `PillarsEngageSteppedTops` requires a complete outward crossing and return, actual trusted top plants on at least two distinct columns per leg, and bounded simulation and posed hip reach. `PaViz_NavigationFocus` plans fourteen real-RHI views of eleven walkers across seven fixtures: these two layouts, separate Crawler4, Crawler6 and Crawler8 upper-ramp approaches before the wall, and Spider on the step field and ledge. Capture completion alone is not foot-contact or chain-clearance proof; inspect the images and actual phase/contact diagnostics. `NeighborCapsulesPreserveGait` samples paired 18-second StepField and Ledge Spider approaches with radii off/on, measuring fresh published segment capsules independently of the selector. It requires matching accepted gait sequences, unchanged body/foot motion and contact state, connected links and foot anchors, non-increasing integrated overlap penalty and an observed strict reduction on each course. It reports the maximum residual overlap instead of promising zero. The intact-approach diagnostic captures all legs of the separate Crawler4/6/8 ramp fixtures; neither its traces nor the paired test prove every rendered crossing has been eliminated. `FootReservationsKeepDistinctLandings` observes the standard Ledge and an isolated same-ideal floor contention for 18 seconds; it checks new trusted plants against existing trusted foot spheres, unchanged continuing anchors, distinct contacts, finite feet and actual Ledge progress. Its published-foot sampling does not by itself prove all intermediate swing-target reservations; pure solver and native touchdown tests cover those admissions.

## Debugger

The CkGameplayDebugger plugin provides the standalone Procedural Animation tool under Systems (`ck.ProceduralAnimationDebugger 1`) and an ECS inspector. `UCk_Utils_ProceduralAnimation_Debug_UE::Get_Snapshot` accepts the body, one of its legs or any other direct lifetime child of the body, and returns the body's accepted solver state (including the cadence speed reference the solver runs with, the reach cadence floor, 0 when no enabled leg bounds it, the count of enabled legs skipped by the floor, and the count of missed landing lifts), the surface motion's grounded, trusted-contact and contact-source state, its height source, wall policy and max step height, the contact pending confirmation (its normal and how long it has been seen) and the obstruction its last substep slid along (with the face's normal), the feet plane the solve published (world point, normal and state), actual probe results, each swinging leg's landing-ground probe, the landing point it was cast under and what it found (`ck::EProceduralGaitLandingGround`), each leg's foothold candidates with their verdicts, the chosen one, its source and whether the plant was occluded, the rays of the last solve (with the rigs' link rays, overlaid when the snapshot is taken), per-leg enabled state and entity identity, each rigged leg's segment and foot transforms and its clearance policy, chain state, stored swivel and crossing-link count, and the body pose's status, offset and, when it conforms, its held and applied conform targets when the body has one. History contains copied values and releases live entity references on world/session teardown. The debugger does not rerun traces. Its preview draws the selected leg's foothold candidates as crosses tinted by verdict, a ring around the chosen one and the line from the hip to it, every leg's landing-ground probe (in the warning colour when it found no ground), the surface motion's pending contact normal, the normal of the face it slid along (while the obstruction reads wall) and the feet plane as a 60 x 60 cm square around its point (Ok while fitted, Warn while held, nothing while none), and it tints a chain its rig reports `Crossing` in the error colour (the snapshot counts the crossing links without naming them, so the whole chain is tinted); its leg rows show the foothold source, the candidate count, an occluded plant and the rig's chain state with its swivel angle or crossing-link count, and its detail lines the rays per solve, the configured height policy alongside the last accepted contact source (which can differ while a feet plane supplies height), the feet plane's state, wall policy, max step height and obstruction. A planted rig foot more than 5 cm from its published foot target is shown as a warning with the measured gap. Its only simulation mutations are the leg actions on the selected leg, Disable/Enable and Detach, issued through `Request_EnableDisable` and `Request_Detach` like any gameplay caller.

### Reachable flight samples

Grounded gait bounds each unplanted output by physical chain reach before recording the emitted pose. The continuous flight projection preserves support-frame height when feasible and constrains XY to the corresponding reach-sphere slice; heights outside vertical reach end at the sphere pole. Reach zero retains the authored curve. The same pure projection applies to zero-time observations, without advancing solver state. This does not move planted contacts, validated landings or reservations. BodyPose includes current published swinging feet in its presentation-only reach projection; SurfaceMotion still paces only trusted stance contacts. This enforces chain reach, not terrain clearance: a projected XY position is not a new world-space collision proof, and an impossible requested lift cannot be preserved.
